    Biology¶

    142 Fleming-tier papers.

    Synthesis¶

    These Fleming-tier biology papers collectively trace a shift from organismal and evolutionary explanation toward molecular, informational, and computational biology. The lineage begins with selection, sexual selection, population structure, social behavior, and quantitative inference, then moves through developmental staging systems for chick and zebrafish, model-organism genetics in Caenorhabditis elegans, and plant tissue culture and transformation. A central paradigm shift is the identification of nucleic acid as the operative genetic material in viral growth, followed by gene regulation, recombinant plasmids, DNA detection, sequencing, PCR, cloning vectors, and transformation protocols. Together these papers mark biology’s conversion into an experimental engineering science: genes could be isolated, amplified, sequenced, moved, silenced, and interpreted across organisms.

    A second dominant lineage is biomedical molecularization. Cancer, AIDS retroviruses, hepatitis C, cystic fibrosis, Huntington’s disease, HER2/neu, leptin, stem cells, monoclonal antibodies, and endothelin show disease being redefined through genes, viruses, cells, and signaling molecules rather than only symptoms or tissues. The later papers connect biology to statistics, computer science, medicine, and information infrastructure: maximum-likelihood phylogenies, bootstrap confidence limits, neighbor joining, neutrality tests, AMOVA, BLAST, KEGG, the Protein Data Bank, Gene Ontology, population-structure inference, genome sequencing, and expression clustering. By the human genome and tumor-expression papers, biology had become a data-rich comparative science, linking evolution, development, ecology, genetics, immunology, oncology, and computational annotation into a common molecular framework.
