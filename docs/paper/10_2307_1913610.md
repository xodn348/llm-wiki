# A Simple, Positive Semi-Definite, Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent Covariance Matrix

## Why this mattered

Newey and West’s 1987 paper mattered because it turned a difficult econometric nuisance into a standard, reliable tool. Before it, inference in time-series and panel-like settings was fragile when regression errors were both heteroskedastic and serially correlated: conventional standard errors could be badly misleading, while existing corrections were either less general, less convenient, or could produce covariance estimates with undesirable numerical properties. The paper’s key contribution was a simple HAC covariance estimator that was positive semi-definite by construction and consistent under broad conditions, making robust inference practical for applied work.

The paradigm shift was not a new model but a new default for inference. After Newey–West, researchers could estimate otherwise familiar regressions while allowing unknown forms of conditional heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation, then report standard errors, confidence intervals, and test statistics with far fewer assumptions about the error process. This helped move empirical economics away from treating spherical errors as a routine maintained assumption and toward robust covariance estimation as part of ordinary statistical practice.

Its influence also lies in how it became infrastructure for later econometrics. HAC estimation underpins large parts of modern time-series regression, generalized method of moments, asset-pricing tests, macroeconomic forecasting evaluation, and empirical finance, where serial dependence and changing volatility are expected rather than exceptional. Subsequent work refined bandwidth choice, kernels, fixed-b asymptotics, long-run variance estimation, and robust inference in dependent data settings, but Newey and West supplied the compact, usable form that made these ideas widely adoptable.

## Abstract

This paper describes a simple method of calculating a heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent covariance matrix that is positive semi-definite by construction. It also establishes consistency of the estimated covariance matrix under fairly general conditions.
