# Absence of Diffusion in Certain Random Lattices

## Why this mattered

Anderson’s 1958 paper mattered because it showed that disorder is not merely a nuisance that scatters waves and reduces transport; in a quantum lattice it can qualitatively eliminate diffusion. The central shift was from treating randomness as a perturbation on extended states to recognizing that sufficiently strong random site energies can produce localized eigenstates, so that an electron, spin excitation, or other quantum amplitude remains confined rather than spreading through the system. This made “absence of diffusion” a coherent physical phase of matter, not just a sign of imperfect samples or inadequate approximations.

The paper also supplied a minimal model in which the phenomenon could be studied sharply: hopping between lattice sites with random energies. That stripped-down formulation made it possible to ask precise questions about when transport begins, how localization depends on disorder and dimensionality, and whether a mobility edge separates localized from extended states. In doing so, Anderson created the conceptual basis for what became Anderson localization, a cornerstone of modern condensed-matter physics and wave physics more broadly.

Subsequent breakthroughs built directly on this change in viewpoint. Scaling theory of localization, the theory of the metal-insulator transition, mesoscopic conductance fluctuations, weak localization, and later studies of localization for light, sound, cold atoms, and many-body systems all inherit the idea that interference in a disordered medium can suppress transport fundamentally. The paper’s lasting importance is that it made disorder an organizing principle for new physics, rather than a correction to be averaged away.

## Abstract

This paper presents a simple model for such processes as spin diffusion or conduction in the "impurity band." These processes involve transport in a lattice which is in some sense random, and in them diffusion is expected to take place via quantum jumps between localized sites. In this simple model the essential randomness is introduced by requiring the energy to vary randomly from site to site. It is shown that at low enough densities no diffusion at all can take place, and the criteria for transport to occur are given.
