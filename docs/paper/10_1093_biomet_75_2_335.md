# Testing for a unit root in time series regression

## Why this mattered

Phillips and Perron’s paper mattered because it broadened unit-root testing beyond the tightly parameterized autoregressive error structures used in the Dickey-Fuller tradition. By making the treatment of nuisance serial correlation and heteroskedasticity nonparametric, the paper gave applied researchers tests that could be used in more realistic macroeconomic and financial time series, where innovations are rarely independent and identically distributed. This shifted unit-root testing from a narrow model-specific exercise toward a robust asymptotic framework for distinguishing stochastic trends from deterministic trend stationarity.

The immediate practical consequence was that questions about persistence, shocks, and long-run equilibria could be studied with fewer assumptions about short-run dynamics. The Phillips-Perron tests became part of the standard empirical toolkit for deciding whether regressions in levels risked spurious inference, whether differencing was appropriate, and whether apparent trends reflected genuine nonstationarity. The paper’s allowance for drift and deterministic time trends was especially important in economics, where many series grow over time but may do so for fundamentally different statistical reasons.

Its longer-run significance lies in how it helped consolidate the modern econometrics of nonstationary time series. Together with Dickey-Fuller testing, Engle-Granger cointegration, and later Johansen system methods, the Phillips-Perron framework made it possible to build empirical models that explicitly separated unit-root behavior, deterministic trends, and long-run equilibrium relations. The paper did not simply add another test; it helped make nonstationarity a central object of econometric theory and practice, setting the stage for much of the subsequent work on cointegration, persistence, and robust inference in time series.

## Abstract

This paper proposes new tests for detecting the presence of a unit root in quite general time series models. Our approach is nonparametric with respect to nuisance parameters and thereby allows for a very wide class of weakly dependent and possibly heterogeneously distributed data. The tests accommodate models with a fitted drift and a time trend so that they may be used to discriminate between unit root nonstationarity and stationarity about a deterministic trend. The limiting distributions of the statistics are obtained under both the unit root null and a sequence of local alternatives. The latter noncentral distribution theory yields local asymptotic power functions for the tests and facilitates comparisons with alternative procedures due to Dickey & Fuller. Simulations are reported on the performance of the new tests in finite samples.
