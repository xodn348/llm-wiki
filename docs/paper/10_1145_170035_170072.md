# Mining association rules between sets of items in large databases

## Why this mattered

This paper helped turn large commercial databases from passive record stores into sources of automatically discoverable patterns. Its central move was to formalize “association rules” over transaction data: statements connecting sets of items that occur together often enough to be considered significant. That framing made a common retail intuition, later popularized as market-basket analysis, into a computational data-mining problem with measurable support and confidence rather than ad hoc reporting.

The paradigm shift was not merely the rule format, but the claim that such rules could be mined efficiently at large scale. By emphasizing pruning, estimation, and buffer management, Agrawal, Imieliński, and Swami made it plausible to search enormous combinatorial spaces of itemsets without enumerating every possibility naively. After this, databases could be used not only to answer known queries, but to generate candidate regularities that analysts had not specified in advance.

The work became a foundation for frequent itemset mining and for later algorithms such as Apriori, which systematized the downward-closure pruning principle and made association-rule mining a core data-mining technique. Its influence also extended beyond retail: the same pattern-discovery template shaped later work in web usage analysis, recommendation systems, bioinformatics, text mining, and exploratory analytics. In historical terms, the paper helped define one of the canonical tasks of the 1990s data-mining era, linking database systems research to the broader shift toward automated knowledge discovery from large datasets.

## Abstract

We are given a large database of customer transactions. Each transaction consists of items purchased by a customer in a visit. We present an efficient algorithm that generates all significant association rules between items in the database. The algorithm incorporates buffer management and novel estimation and pruning techniques. We also present results of applying this algorithm to sales data obtained from a large retailing company, which shows the effectiveness of the algorithm.
