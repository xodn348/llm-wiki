# Deep Learning with Differential Privacy

## Why this mattered

Before this paper, differential privacy was largely associated with databases, statistics, and simpler learning settings; training modern deep neural networks under a usable privacy guarantee was not yet a standard, practical recipe. Abadi et al. showed that non-convex deep learning could be made compatible with differential privacy by modifying stochastic gradient descent: clip per-example gradients, add calibrated Gaussian noise, and track privacy loss with a sharper accountant. The paradigm shift was that privacy became an optimization-layer property of training itself, not merely a post-hoc restriction on releasing data or model outputs.

This made it newly plausible to train useful neural networks on sensitive, large-scale datasets while giving a formal bound on how much any one training example could influence the released model. Just as importantly, the paper translated differential privacy into engineering terms familiar to deep learning practitioners: SGD, minibatches, gradient norms, noise multipliers, model accuracy, and training cost. That bridge helped move privacy-preserving machine learning from a theoretical guarantee toward an implementable discipline, later reflected in widely used DP-SGD variants and libraries.

Its influence is visible in subsequent work on private federated learning, private language-model training, membership-inference defenses, and empirical privacy auditing. The paper did not solve the full utility cost of privacy, especially for very large models or scarce data, but it established the central baseline against which later private deep learning systems were measured. After it, the question was no longer whether deep networks could be trained with formal differential privacy at all, but how far the privacy-utility-compute frontier could be pushed.

## Abstract

Machine learning techniques based on neural networks are achieving remarkable results in a wide variety of domains. Often, the training of models requires large, representative datasets, which may be crowdsourced and contain sensitive information. The models should not expose private information in these datasets. Addressing this goal, we develop new algorithmic techniques for learning and a refined analysis of privacy costs within the framework of differential privacy. Our implementation and experiments demonstrate that we can train deep neural networks with non-convex objectives, under a modest privacy budget, and at a manageable cost in software complexity, training efficiency, and model quality.
