# Role of YAP/TAZ in mechanotransduction

## Why this mattered

Before Dupont et al., YAP and TAZ were understood mainly as downstream transcriptional effectors of the Hippo growth-control pathway. This paper shifted that framing by showing that they also act as sensors and mediators of mechanical context: extracellular-matrix rigidity, cell spreading, cell geometry, and cytoskeletal tension regulated whether YAP/TAZ entered the nucleus and drove transcription. The key conceptual move was to connect a canonical organ-size and cancer pathway to the physical state of the cell, making mechanics a direct input into gene regulation rather than a background condition.

That made it possible to study mechanotransduction with a concrete molecular readout and intervention point. Instead of treating stiffness, adhesion, or shape as diffuse biophysical influences, researchers could ask how these cues controlled YAP/TAZ localization, TEAD-dependent transcription, survival, proliferation, and differentiation. The paper also showed functional consequences: YAP/TAZ were required for stiffness-dependent mesenchymal stem-cell differentiation and geometry-dependent endothelial-cell survival, while activated YAP could override physical constraints. This helped explain how identical genomes can produce different cell fates in different mechanical niches.

Its influence is visible in later work on stem-cell fate, fibrosis, regeneration, organoids, cancer progression, and tissue engineering, where YAP/TAZ became a central bridge between matrix mechanics and transcriptional programs. Subsequent breakthroughs refined the pathway through actomyosin tension, Rho signalling, focal adhesions, nuclear mechanics, and Hippo-independent regulation, but the 2011 Nature paper established the paradigm: cells do not merely receive biochemical instructions; they convert the physical architecture of their environment into durable changes in gene expression and behavior. Source: [Nature article](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature10137).
