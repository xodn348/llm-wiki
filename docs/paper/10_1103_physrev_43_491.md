# The Positive Electron

## Why this mattered

Anderson’s paper made antimatter an experimental object rather than a theoretical possibility. Dirac’s relativistic electron theory had already implied a positively charged counterpart to the electron, but Anderson’s cloud-chamber photographs provided direct evidence for a light, positively charged particle distinct from the proton. The key shift was empirical: charge sign, curvature in a magnetic field, ionization, and energy loss could be combined to identify a new elementary particle in cosmic radiation.

The discovery changed particle physics from a field centered on electrons, protons, photons, and nuclei into one in which new particles could be found and classified by tracks, interactions, and decay products. It also validated the idea that relativistic quantum theory could predict wholly unfamiliar forms of matter. After this paper, cloud chambers and later emulsions, bubble chambers, and electronic detectors became discovery instruments for an expanding particle zoo, including the muon, pion, kaon, and strange particles.

The positron also opened the route to pair production, annihilation physics, and eventually antimatter as a central concept in quantum field theory. Its identification helped make charge conjugation, particle-antiparticle symmetry, and vacuum pair creation part of the working language of high-energy physics. Later breakthroughs in quantum electrodynamics, accelerator physics, medical positron-emission tomography, and cosmological questions about the matter-antimatter asymmetry all trace part of their experimental and conceptual ancestry to this observation.

## Abstract

Out of a group of 1300 photographs of cosmic-ray tracks in a vertical Wilson chamber 15 tracks were of positive particles which could not have a mass as great as that of the proton. From an examination of the energy-loss and ionization produced it is concluded that the charge is less than twice, and is probably exactly equal to, that of the proton. If these particles carry unit positive charge the curvatures and ionizations produced require the mass to be less than twenty times the electron mass. These particles will be called positrons. Because they occur in groups associated with other tracks it is concluded that they must be secondary particles ejected from atomic nuclei.
