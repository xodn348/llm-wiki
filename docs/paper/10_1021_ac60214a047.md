# Smoothing and Differentiation of Data by Simplified Least Squares Procedures.

## Why this mattered

Savitzky and Golay’s 1964 paper mattered because it changed smoothing from a blunt act of noise suppression into a local model-fitting procedure that could preserve scientifically meaningful structure. Instead of replacing each point by a simple moving average, they showed how to fit low-degree polynomials over a sliding window by least squares and tabulate the resulting convolution coefficients. The practical consequence was decisive for spectroscopy and analytical chemistry: noisy digitized curves could be smoothed while retaining peak height, width, area, and position far better than many earlier averaging methods.

The paper also made numerical differentiation of experimental data routine rather than exceptional. Derivatives of spectra are highly useful for locating overlapping peaks, resolving shoulders, and emphasizing subtle features, but differentiation amplifies noise. Savitzky and Golay’s procedure linked smoothing and differentiation in the same least-squares framework, making derivative spectra usable in ordinary laboratory analysis. This helped turn increasing availability of digital instruments and computers into a new style of data treatment: raw measurements were no longer just plotted or manually interpreted, but algorithmically transformed while preserving quantitative content.

Its later importance extends well beyond the original analytical-chemistry setting. The “Savitzky-Golay filter” became a standard tool wherever sampled signals contain local shape information that should not be washed out: spectroscopy, chromatography, mass spectrometry, electrochemistry, biomechanics, astronomy, image processing, and time-series analysis. Its paradigm shift was not that it introduced least squares itself, but that it compressed local polynomial regression into simple reusable filter coefficients, giving scientists a computationally cheap way to denoise and differentiate data without discarding the very features they were trying to measure.
