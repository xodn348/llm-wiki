# The Sloan Digital Sky Survey: Technical Summary

## Why this mattered

Before SDSS, much of observational cosmology depended on heterogeneous photographic catalogs or targeted surveys that were powerful but limited in scale, calibration uniformity, and reproducibility. York et al. made explicit a different model: astronomy as a large, homogeneous, digital data system. By combining calibrated five-band imaging over π steradians with planned spectroscopy for roughly one million galaxies and 100,000 quasars, the survey would turn large-scale structure, galaxy demographics, quasar evolution, and Milky Way substructure into statistically industrial problems rather than collections of bespoke observations.

The shift was not only larger numbers. SDSS tied imaging, astrometry, photometry, target selection, spectroscopy, pipelines, and public data products into a single technical architecture. That made it possible to select objects from a uniform catalog, obtain spectra at scale, and compare results across the sky with well-characterized systematics. The paper therefore mattered as a blueprint for a new kind of astronomical instrument: not just a telescope or camera, but an end-to-end survey machine whose data could be reused by researchers far beyond the original collaboration.

Many subsequent breakthroughs rested on that infrastructure. SDSS enabled precise three-dimensional maps of the nearby universe, major measurements of galaxy clustering and baryon acoustic oscillations, large quasar samples for studying cosmic history, systematic studies of galaxy properties, and discoveries in Galactic archaeology such as stellar streams and halo structure. Its public releases also helped normalize open, database-driven astronomy, influencing later surveys that treated calibrated pipelines and accessible archives as central scientific products rather than secondary documentation.

## Abstract

The Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) will provide the data to support detailed investigations of the distribution of luminous and non- luminous matter in the Universe: a photometrically and astrometrically calibrated digital imaging survey of pi steradians above about Galactic latitude 30 degrees in five broad optical bands to a depth of g' about 23 magnitudes, and a spectroscopic survey of the approximately one million brightest galaxies and 10^5 brightest quasars found in the photometric object catalog produced by the imaging survey. This paper summarizes the observational parameters and data products of the SDSS, and serves as an introduction to extensive technical on-line documentation.
