# Prediction of Creatinine Clearance from Serum Creatinine

## Why this mattered

Cockcroft and Gault’s formula mattered because it turned kidney function estimation from a cumbersome measurement into a bedside calculation. Before this work, creatinine clearance usually required timed urine collections, which were inconvenient, error-prone, and poorly suited to routine prescribing decisions. By showing that serum creatinine could be interpreted through age, body weight, and sex, the paper made it possible to estimate renal clearance from ordinary clinical data rather than from a 24-hour collection. The key shift was not that serum creatinine alone became reliable, but that its limitations could be partly corrected by embedding it in a physiological model of creatinine production.

That change made renal function operational in everyday medicine. Drug dosing, especially for renally cleared or nephrotoxic drugs, could now be adjusted systematically at the point of care. The formula became embedded in clinical pharmacology, nephrology, and trial eligibility criteria because it offered a reproducible estimate of creatinine clearance using variables already available in most adult patients. Its influence also reflected a broader methodological transition: kidney function became something that could be standardized, compared, and acted on across institutions, not merely measured case by case.

The paper also set the template for later estimated kidney-function equations. Subsequent formulas such as MDRD and CKD-EPI improved population calibration and shifted emphasis toward estimated glomerular filtration rate, but they built on the same central premise: serum creatinine becomes clinically powerful when interpreted with demographic and body-size covariates. Cockcroft-Gault was eventually recognized as imperfect, particularly across extremes of body composition and in modern standardized creatinine assays, yet its historical importance lies in making quantitative renal assessment routine, scalable, and directly connected to therapeutic decisions.

## Abstract

A formula has been developed to predict creatinine clearance (C&lt;sub&gt;cr&lt;/sub&gt;) from serum creatinine (S&lt;sub&gt;cr&lt;/sub&gt;) in adult males: Ccr = (140 – age) (wt kg)/72 × S&lt;sub&gt;cr&lt;/sub&gt;(mg/100ml) (15% less in females). Derivation included the relationship found between age and 24-hour creatinine excretion/kg in 249 patients aged 18–92. Values for C&lt;sub&gt;cr&lt;/sub&gt; were predicted by this formula and four other methods and the results compared with the means of two 24-hour C&lt;sub&gt;cr’s&lt;/sub&gt; measured in 236 patients. The above formula gave a correlation coefficient between predicted and mean measured Ccr·s of 0.83; on average, the difference between predicted and mean measured values was no greater than that between paired clearances. Factors for age and body weight must be included for reasonable prediction.
