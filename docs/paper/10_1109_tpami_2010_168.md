# Single Image Haze Removal Using Dark Channel Prior

## Why this mattered

Before this paper, haze removal was usually treated as an underconstrained problem unless multiple images, polarization cues, known scene depth, or strong assumptions about the scene were available. He, Sun, and Tang showed that a simple statistical regularity of natural outdoor images, the “dark channel prior,” could supply enough constraint to estimate transmission from a single hazy image under the atmospheric scattering model. This shifted dehazing from a specialized capture problem into a broadly applicable single-image restoration problem.

The key contribution was not merely better visual results, but a new way to extract scene structure from ordinary photographs. By linking low-intensity pixels in local haze-free patches to haze thickness, the method made it possible to recover both a dehazed image and an approximate depth map from one input image. That depth-byproduct mattered because it showed that image priors could reveal latent physical properties of a scene, not just improve appearance.

Its influence extended beyond dehazing. The paper became a canonical example of a hand-crafted natural image prior that was simple, interpretable, and surprisingly powerful. Later learning-based dehazing methods often used it as a benchmark, a training signal, or a conceptual foil: deep networks eventually learned richer priors from data, but the dark channel prior helped establish the problem setting, the atmospheric-model formulation, and the expectation that single-image restoration could infer hidden scene variables from statistical regularities alone.

## Abstract

In this paper, we propose a simple but effective image prior-dark channel prior to remove haze from a single input image. The dark channel prior is a kind of statistics of outdoor haze-free images. It is based on a key observation-most local patches in outdoor haze-free images contain some pixels whose intensity is very low in at least one color channel. Using this prior with the haze imaging model, we can directly estimate the thickness of the haze and recover a high-quality haze-free image. Results on a variety of hazy images demonstrate the power of the proposed prior. Moreover, a high-quality depth map can also be obtained as a byproduct of haze removal.
