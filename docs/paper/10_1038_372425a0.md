# Positional cloning of the mouse obese gene and its human homologue

## Why this mattered

Before this paper, severe obesity in the `ob/ob` mouse was known genetically but not mechanistically: a single recessive mutation produced massive hyperphagia, reduced energy expenditure, diabetes-like physiology, and infertility, yet the molecular signal was unknown. Zhang and colleagues positionally cloned the mouse `ob` gene and identified its human homologue, showing that the gene encoded a conserved, adipose-expressed protein disrupted in the obese mouse. This changed obesity biology from a largely behavioral or nonspecific metabolic problem into a tractable molecular physiology problem: body fat could be understood as regulated by definable genes, circulating signals, and feedback pathways.

The deeper paradigm shift was the recognition that adipose tissue was not merely passive energy storage. The `ob` gene product, soon named leptin, became the prototype adipocyte-derived hormone: a signal from fat stores to the brain and other tissues. Once the gene was cloned, researchers could produce the protein, measure it, test replacement in mutant mice, identify its receptor, and map the hypothalamic circuits that regulate appetite, energy expenditure, reproduction, and endocrine adaptation to starvation. Those steps rapidly connected classical mouse genetics to human metabolic disease and opened the modern field of adipokine biology.

The paper did not make common human obesity a simple single-gene disease; later work showed that leptin deficiency is rare and that most obesity involves leptin resistance or more complex polygenic and environmental causes. Its importance lies instead in making energy homeostasis molecularly addressable. The cloning of `ob` provided the entry point for leptin therapy in rare congenital leptin deficiency, for the discovery of the leptin receptor and melanocortin pathway disorders, and for a broader shift toward viewing obesity as a regulated neuroendocrine disease rather than merely a failure of willpower.
