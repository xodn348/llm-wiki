# The ERA5 global reanalysis

## Why this mattered

ERA5 mattered because it made global weather and climate history usable at a new level of practical detail. Earlier reanalyses had already fused observations with numerical weather prediction models into coherent retrospective records, but ERA5 sharply raised the standard: roughly 31 km horizontal resolution, hourly output, improved data assimilation and model physics, and an ensemble-based uncertainty estimate. That combination changed reanalysis from a specialist climate diagnostic into a general-purpose infrastructure dataset for meteorology, hydrology, renewable energy, air quality, extremes research, and climate-risk assessment.

The paradigm shift was not simply “better resolution.” ERA5 provided a physically consistent, globally complete, observation-constrained record that could resolve the evolution of storms, waves, precipitation, temperature, humidity, and circulation with enough temporal granularity to support event-scale analysis. The paper’s reported gains over ERA-Interim, including improved forecast skill, better fit to many assimilated observations, improved ocean-wave agreement, and higher monthly precipitation correlation with GPCP, gave users evidence that the dataset was not just more detailed but more reliable for many applied purposes. Its uncertainty estimate also made explicit that reanalysis quality varies with the observing system, an essential point for long-term climate interpretation.

After ERA5, many later breakthroughs in climate services and applied Earth-system science became easier to operationalize: attribution studies of extreme events, global renewable-resource assessments, flood and drought monitoring, urban heat and compound-risk analysis, machine-learning weather models trained on reanalysis fields, and high-resolution climate-impact products all gained a common reference backbone. ERA5 did not replace direct observations or climate-model projections, but it became one of the principal bridges between them: a gridded, dynamically consistent account of the recent atmosphere that allowed researchers to connect physical mechanisms, historical events, and decision-relevant risk at global scale.

## Abstract

Abstract Within the Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S), ECMWF is producing the ERA5 reanalysis which, once completed, will embody a detailed record of the global atmosphere, land surface and ocean waves from 1950 onwards. This new reanalysis replaces the ERA‐Interim reanalysis (spanning 1979 onwards) which was started in 2006. ERA5 is based on the Integrated Forecasting System (IFS) Cy41r2 which was operational in 2016. ERA5 thus benefits from a decade of developments in model physics, core dynamics and data assimilation. In addition to a significantly enhanced horizontal resolution of 31 km, compared to 80 km for ERA‐Interim, ERA5 has hourly output throughout, and an uncertainty estimate from an ensemble (3‐hourly at half the horizontal resolution). This paper describes the general set‐up of ERA5, as well as a basic evaluation of characteristics and performance, with a focus on the dataset from 1979 onwards which is currently publicly available. Re‐forecasts from ERA5 analyses show a gain of up to one day in skill with respect to ERA‐Interim. Comparison with radiosonde and PILOT data prior to assimilation shows an improved fit for temperature, wind and humidity in the troposphere, but not the stratosphere. A comparison with independent buoy data shows a much improved fit for ocean wave height. The uncertainty estimate reflects the evolution of the observing systems used in ERA5. The enhanced temporal and spatial resolution allows for a detailed evolution of weather systems. For precipitation, global‐mean correlation with monthly‐mean GPCP data is increased from 67% to 77%. In general, low‐frequency variability is found to be well represented and from 10 hPa downwards general patterns of anomalies in temperature match those from the ERA‐Interim, MERRA‐2 and JRA‐55 reanalyses.
