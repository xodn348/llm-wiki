# Two-dimensional gas of massless Dirac fermions in graphene

## Why this mattered

This paper helped establish graphene not merely as an atomically thin carbon sheet, but as a platform for relativistic quantum physics in a tabletop solid-state system. Novoselov, Geim, and colleagues showed that charge carriers in single-layer graphene behave as effectively massless Dirac fermions, producing an unusual “half-integer” quantum Hall effect and a Berry phase of π. That result was a conceptual break from ordinary semiconductor two-dimensional electron gases, where electrons are described by a parabolic energy band and conventional effective mass. Graphene instead made band topology, chirality, and relativistic-like quasiparticles experimentally accessible through transport measurements.

What became newly possible was a controllable material system in which relativistic quantum phenomena could be studied without particle accelerators or exotic high-energy conditions. By tuning carrier density with a gate voltage, researchers could move continuously between electron and hole conduction while preserving high mobility, allowing direct exploration of ambipolar transport, Klein-tunneling-related physics, anomalous quantum Hall behavior, and disorder effects in a Dirac spectrum. The paper also gave the wider condensed-matter community a clear experimental signature that graphene’s electronic structure was not just theoretically elegant, but measurable and robust.

Its influence extended well beyond graphene itself. The work helped launch the broader field of two-dimensional materials and sharpened interest in Dirac and topological quasiparticles across condensed matter physics, including topological insulators, Weyl and Dirac semimetals, and van der Waals heterostructures. Together with the contemporaneous experimental isolation of graphene and related quantum Hall observations, it was central to the rapid elevation of graphene into a paradigm system, contributing directly to the scientific trajectory recognized by the 2010 Nobel Prize in Physics awarded to Geim and Novoselov.
