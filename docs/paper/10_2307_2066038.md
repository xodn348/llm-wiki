# Outline of a Theory of Practice.

## Why this mattered

*Outline of a Theory of Practice* mattered because it gave social science a durable way out of the opposition between structural determinism and individual voluntarism. Bourdieu’s central move was to treat practice as generated by historically formed dispositions, or *habitus*, operating within structured social conditions rather than by explicit rules, conscious calculation, or mechanical reproduction alone. This made it possible to explain patterned social action without reducing actors either to rule-following cultural automatons or to fully transparent rational choosers.

The paradigm shift was methodological as well as theoretical: kinship, ritual, honor, exchange, and domination could now be analyzed as practical strategies embedded in fields of power, with symbolic capital and symbolic violence treated as real mechanisms of social reproduction. Later work in sociology, anthropology, education, cultural studies, science studies, and political sociology built directly on this vocabulary of habitus, field, capital, doxa, and practice. Subsequent breakthroughs in the study of taste, schooling, class reproduction, professions, cultural production, and everyday embodiment became newly tractable because Bourdieu supplied a framework for connecting micro-level conduct to macro-level structures without collapsing one into the other.
