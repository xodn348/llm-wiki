# The representative concentration pathways: an overview

## Why this mattered

The paradigm shift was that the RCPs separated climate-model experiments from a single prescribed socioeconomic storyline. Earlier scenario families, especially the SRES scenarios, bundled emissions with detailed assumptions about population, technology, economic growth, and policy. Van Vuuren and colleagues instead defined pathways by their radiative forcing outcomes in 2100, from the strong-mitigation RCP2.6 to the high-forcing RCP8.5, and supplied harmonized emissions, concentrations, land-use, and air-pollution data at resolutions climate and Earth-system models could directly use. This made scenarios into a common experimental protocol: different modeling groups could ask comparable questions about climate response under the same forcing pathways.

That design made a new division of labor possible. Integrated assessment models could produce internally consistent emissions and land-use pathways; climate models could translate those pathways into temperature, precipitation, ocean, cryosphere, and carbon-cycle outcomes; impacts researchers could then use the resulting climate projections in a coordinated way. The paper therefore helped turn scenario analysis from a set of loosely comparable narratives into shared infrastructure for CMIP5, the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report, and a large body of mitigation, impacts, adaptation, and detection-attribution research.

Its influence also lies in what it enabled next. The RCP framework became the bridge to later scenario systems, especially the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways, which recombined socioeconomic futures with forcing targets rather than treating either as fixed. Subsequent breakthroughs in probabilistic climate-risk assessment, regional impact modeling, carbon-budget analysis, and policy-relevant comparisons of 1.5 C, 2 C, and higher-warming worlds depended on this kind of standardized scenario architecture. The paper mattered because it made climate futures interoperable: not predictions, but carefully specified pathways that let many disciplines study the same possible worlds.

## Abstract

This paper summarizes the development process and main characteristics of the Representative Concentration Pathways (RCPs), a set of four new pathways developed for the climate modeling community as a basis for long-term and near-term modeling experiments. The four RCPs together span the range of year 2100 radiative forcing values found in the open literature, i.e. from 2.6 to 8.5 W/m2. The RCPs are the product of an innovative collaboration between integrated assessment modelers, climate modelers, terrestrial ecosystem modelers and emission inventory experts. The resulting product forms a comprehensive data set with high spatial and sectoral resolutions for the period extending to 2100. Land use and emissions of air pollutants and greenhouse gases are reported mostly at a 0.5 × 0.5 degree spatial resolution, with air pollutants also provided per sector (for well-mixed gases, a coarser resolution is used). The underlying integrated assessment model outputs for land use, atmospheric emissions and concentration data were harmonized across models and scenarios to ensure consistency with historical observations while preserving individual scenario trends. For most variables, the RCPs cover a wide range of the existing literature. The RCPs are supplemented with extensions (Extended Concentration Pathways, ECPs), which allow climate modeling experiments through the year 2300. The RCPs are an important development in climate research and provide a potential foundation for further research and assessment, including emissions mitigation and impact analysis.
