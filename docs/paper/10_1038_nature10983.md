# The genomic and transcriptomic architecture of 2,000 breast tumours reveals novel subgroups

## Why this mattered

Before METABRIC, breast cancer was already understood as heterogeneous, especially through ER/HER2 pathology and expression-based “intrinsic” subtypes. This paper shifted the scale and logic of classification: by integrating copy-number alteration, gene-expression, and long-term clinical outcome data across about 2,000 tumours, it showed that breast cancer could be stratified into reproducible “integrative clusters” with distinct biology and prognosis. The key change was not just more subtypes, but a framework in which genomic architecture itself helped define disease entities.

That made it possible to ask which DNA alterations were merely markers and which appeared to shape tumour transcriptional programs. The study linked cis-acting and trans-acting copy-number events to subgroup-specific gene networks, revealing that clinically familiar categories such as ER-positive or HER2-positive disease contained deeper molecular divisions. In practical terms, it moved the field toward cohort-scale, data-integrated cancer taxonomy: classification by jointly considering genome, transcriptome, and outcome rather than by any single assay layer.

Its influence is visible in later breast-cancer multi-omics, TCGA-style molecular atlases, cBioPortal-era comparative genomics, and the continuing effort to refine prognostic and therapeutic groups beyond traditional receptor status. Not all METABRIC clusters became direct clinical categories, but the paper helped establish the modern premise that large, clinically annotated tumour cohorts can uncover biologically meaningful subgroups that smaller expression-only studies could not resolve.
