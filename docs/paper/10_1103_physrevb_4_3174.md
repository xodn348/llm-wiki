# Renormalization Group and Critical Phenomena. I. Renormalization Group and the Kadanoff Scaling Picture

## Why this mattered

Wilson’s paper helped turn scaling near critical points from a phenomenological observation into a dynamical framework. Kadanoff’s block-spin picture had suggested why systems at different length scales could look statistically similar near a critical point, and Widom-Kadanoff scaling laws summarized striking regularities among critical exponents. Wilson’s contribution was to recast that intuition as renormalization-group flow: differential equations describing how coupling parameters change as short-distance degrees of freedom are systematically absorbed. In this form, critical behavior was no longer just a set of empirical power laws, but the consequence of trajectories in a space of theories.

The decisive conceptual move was the fixed point. The paper showed that scaling laws follow naturally when the renormalization-group equations approach a fixed point, with “irrelevant” variables dying away at long distances. This explained universality: microscopically different magnets, fluids, or lattice models could share the same critical exponents because their long-distance behavior was governed by the same fixed point and the same relevant directions away from it. That shifted attention from exact microscopic details to the structure of flows, stability, and scale dependence.

This framework made later breakthroughs possible across statistical physics and quantum field theory. Wilson’s subsequent work used it to compute critical exponents and organize the epsilon expansion, while the broader renormalization-group viewpoint became central to effective field theory: physics at one scale can be predictive without complete knowledge of shorter-distance details. The paper therefore mattered not only because it clarified critical phenomena, but because it supplied a general language for emergence, universality, and scale-dependent laws in modern theoretical physics.

## Abstract

The Kadanoff theory of scaling near the critical point for an Ising ferromagnet is cast in differential form. The resulting differential equations are an example of the differential equations of the renormalization group. It is shown that the Widom-Kadanoff scaling laws arise naturally from these differential equations if the coefficients in the equations are analytic at the critical point. A generalization of the Kadanoff scaling picture involving an "irrelevant" variable is considered; in this case the scaling laws result from the renormalization-group equations only if the solution of the equations goes asymptotically to a fixed point.
