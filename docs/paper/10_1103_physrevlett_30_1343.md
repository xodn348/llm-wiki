# Ultraviolet Behavior of Non-Abelian Gauge Theories

## Why this mattered

Gross and Wilczek’s paper helped overturn the expectation that quantum field theory could not describe the strong nuclear force at short distances. By showing that non-Abelian gauge theories can become effectively weakly coupled at high energies, they identified *asymptotic freedom*: the counterintuitive behavior in which quarks behave almost like free particles when probed at very small distances. This gave a dynamical explanation for the approximate Bjorken scaling seen in deep-inelastic scattering, where electrons appeared to scatter from pointlike constituents inside protons and neutrons.

The result made a realistic gauge theory of the strong interaction newly plausible. Before this work, the parton model described scattering data phenomenologically, but it lacked a firm field-theoretic foundation. Asymptotic freedom showed how quarks could appear free in high-energy experiments while still being strongly bound at ordinary distances, providing the conceptual bridge to quantum chromodynamics. In combination with related work by Politzer, it established non-Abelian gauge theory as the central framework for strong-interaction physics.

Its importance extended beyond explaining one class of experiments. The paper turned the renormalization group from a source of trouble for strong interactions into a predictive tool, making high-energy strong-interaction processes calculable through logarithmic corrections. It also helped consolidate the Standard Model’s architecture: non-Abelian gauge symmetry was no longer merely mathematically elegant, but empirically indispensable. Subsequent breakthroughs in QCD, including scaling violations, jet physics, and precision tests of the running strong coupling, all depend on the mechanism this paper revealed.

## Abstract

It is shown that a wide class of non-Abelian gauge theories have, up to calculable logarithmic corrections, free-field-theory asymptotic behavior. It is suggested that Bjorken scaling may be obtained from strong-interaction dynamics based on non-Abelian gauge symmetry.
