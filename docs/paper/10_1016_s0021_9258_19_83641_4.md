# A new generation of Ca2+ indicators with greatly improved fluorescence properties.

## Why this mattered

Before this work, intracellular calcium could be measured with fluorescent chelators such as quin2, but the measurements were dim, technically demanding, and often best suited to bulk cell populations. Grynkiewicz, Poenie, and Tsien’s indicators, especially fura-2 and indo-1, changed that by making Ca²⁺ signals much brighter, more selective, and spectrally responsive to binding. The key shift was not simply better sensitivity: Ca²⁺ binding altered excitation or emission wavelength, enabling ratiometric measurements that corrected for dye concentration, cell thickness, illumination, leakage, and photobleaching. That made quantitative calcium imaging in living single cells far more reliable.

This mattered because Ca²⁺ is a universal intracellular messenger, but its biological meaning often depends on where, when, and how strongly it changes. The new indicators made it practical to watch calcium dynamics in individual cells, adherent cultures, tissue preparations, and eventually complex physiological systems. After this paper, calcium signaling could be studied as a spatial and temporal process rather than as an averaged biochemical endpoint. Waves, oscillations, localized transients, and stimulus-specific calcium signatures became experimentally accessible across neuroscience, immunology, muscle physiology, fertilization, secretion, and developmental biology.

The paper also helped establish a model for modern biological imaging: build chemical probes whose optical properties report molecular state inside living systems. Tsien’s later work on fluorescent reporters and genetically encoded indicators grew from the same conceptual lineage, even as protein-based sensors eventually complemented and displaced many synthetic dyes in some settings. The 1985 indicators were therefore not just improved reagents; they helped move cell biology toward live, quantitative, optical measurement of signaling in its native context.

## Abstract

A new family of highly fluorescent indicators has been synthesized for biochemical studies of the physiological role of cytosolic free Ca2+. The compounds combine an 8-coordinate tetracarboxylate chelating site with stilbene chromophores. Incorporation of the ethylenic linkage of the stilbene into a heterocyclic ring enhances the quantum efficiency and photochemical stability of the fluorophore. Compared to their widely used predecessor, "quin2", the new dyes offer up to 30-fold brighter fluorescence, major changes in wavelength not just intensity upon Ca2+ binding, slightly lower affinities for Ca2+, slightly longer wavelengths of excitation, and considerably improved selectivity for Ca2+ over other divalent cations. These properties, particularly the wavelength sensitivity to Ca2+, should make these dyes the preferred fluorescent indicators for many intracellular applications, especially in single cells, adherent cell layers, or bulk tissues.
