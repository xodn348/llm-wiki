# Empagliflozin, Cardiovascular Outcomes, and Mortality in Type 2 Diabetes

## Why this mattered

Before EMPA-REG OUTCOME, glucose-lowering drugs for type 2 diabetes were largely judged by whether they reduced hemoglobin A1c without causing unacceptable harm, and cardiovascular outcome trials were designed mainly to exclude excess risk. This paper changed that frame. In a high-risk population already receiving standard care, empagliflozin did not merely show cardiovascular safety; it reduced the primary composite cardiovascular outcome and, more strikingly, lowered cardiovascular death, hospitalization for heart failure, and all-cause mortality. The pattern mattered because the largest effects were not on myocardial infarction or stroke, the classic atherosclerotic endpoints, but on death and heart-failure hospitalization, suggesting that SGLT2 inhibition was doing something clinically important beyond conventional glucose control.

That result made a new therapeutic category possible: diabetes drugs could be selected for organ protection, not just glycemic efficacy. It helped move clinical practice from a glucose-centric model toward risk-based treatment in which cardiovascular disease, heart failure, and kidney risk shape drug choice. The trial also gave regulators, guideline panels, and clinicians a concrete example of a glucose-lowering agent producing hard-outcome benefits on top of contemporary background therapy, which raised expectations for subsequent cardiovascular and renal outcome trials across the SGLT2 inhibitor class.

Its influence extended beyond diabetes. Later trials of SGLT2 inhibitors in heart failure and chronic kidney disease tested the implication that these drugs acted through mechanisms relevant to cardiorenal physiology, not only through lowering blood glucose. Those studies helped establish SGLT2 inhibitors as foundational therapies for heart failure and kidney protection, including in some populations without diabetes. In that sense, EMPA-REG OUTCOME was paradigm-shifting because it turned a diabetes medication into the leading edge of a broader cardiorenal treatment strategy.

## Abstract

BACKGROUND: The effects of empagliflozin, an inhibitor of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2, in addition to standard care, on cardiovascular morbidity and mortality in patients with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk are not known. METHODS: We randomly assigned patients to receive 10 mg or 25 mg of empagliflozin or placebo once daily. The primary composite outcome was death from cardiovascular causes, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke, as analyzed in the pooled empagliflozin group versus the placebo group. The key secondary composite outcome was the primary outcome plus hospitalization for unstable angina. RESULTS: A total of 7020 patients were treated (median observation time, 3.1 years). The primary outcome occurred in 490 of 4687 patients (10.5%) in the pooled empagliflozin group and in 282 of 2333 patients (12.1%) in the placebo group (hazard ratio in the empagliflozin group, 0.86; 95.02% confidence interval, 0.74 to 0.99; P=0.04 for superiority). There were no significant between-group differences in the rates of myocardial infarction or stroke, but in the empagliflozin group there were significantly lower rates of death from cardiovascular causes (3.7%, vs. 5.9% in the placebo group; 38% relative risk reduction), hospitalization for heart failure (2.7% and 4.1%, respectively; 35% relative risk reduction), and death from any cause (5.7% and 8.3%, respectively; 32% relative risk reduction). There was no significant between-group difference in the key secondary outcome (P=0.08 for superiority). Among patients receiving empagliflozin, there was an increased rate of genital infection but no increase in other adverse events. CONCLUSIONS: Patients with type 2 diabetes at high risk for cardiovascular events who received empagliflozin, as compared with placebo, had a lower rate of the primary composite cardiovascular outcome and of death from any cause when the study drug was added to standard care. (Funded by Boehringer Ingelheim and Eli Lilly; EMPA-REG OUTCOME ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT01131676.).
