# Segment Anything

## Why this mattered

Before *Segment Anything*, segmentation was usually treated as a task-specific supervised problem: train or fine-tune a model for the category, dataset, or annotation style of interest. SAM reframed segmentation as a promptable foundation-model capability. A user could indicate an object with points, boxes, masks, or related prompts, and the model would return plausible masks without task-specific retraining. That made segmentation feel less like a closed benchmark problem and more like a general visual interface primitive.

The paper mattered because it coupled this new task formulation with scale. SA-1B, with over 1 billion masks on 11 million licensed, privacy-respecting images, was not just a larger dataset; it was produced through a model-in-the-loop data engine that made broad mask supervision economically and technically feasible. This shifted the field’s expectations about what a reusable vision model could provide: not semantic understanding by itself, but a high-quality object boundary prior that could be composed with detectors, language models, annotation tools, robotics systems, medical-imaging workflows, remote-sensing pipelines, and creative-editing interfaces.

Its influence is visible in the wave of “SAM-for-X” work and in later promptable segmentation systems, including extensions to video such as SAM 2. The central breakthrough was composability: once mask generation became a general service, other systems could ask *what* to segment using language, detection, tracking, or human interaction, while SAM handled *where* the object was at pixel level. That separation helped make open-vocabulary and interactive visual understanding much more practical after 2023.

## Abstract

We introduce the Segment Anything (SA) project: a new task, model, and dataset for image segmentation. Using our efficient model in a data collection loop, we built the largest segmentation dataset to date (by far), with over 1 billion masks on 11M licensed and privacy respecting images. The model is designed and trained to be promptable, so it can transfer zero-shot to new image distributions and tasks. We evaluate its capabilities on numerous tasks and find that its zero-shot performance is impressive – often competitive with or even superior to prior fully supervised results. We are releasing the Segment Anything Model (SAM) and corresponding dataset (SA-1B) of 1B masks and 11M images at segment-anything.com to foster research into foundation models for computer vision. We recommend reading the full paper at: arxiv.org/abs/2304.02643.
