# Visible-Light Photocatalysis in Nitrogen-Doped Titanium Oxides

## Why this mattered

Before this paper, titanium dioxide was already a central photocatalyst, but its wide band gap meant that practical activity depended mainly on ultraviolet light, a small fraction of sunlight and an even smaller component of indoor illumination. Asahi and colleagues reframed the problem by showing that nitrogen incorporation into TiO₂ could extend absorption into the visible range while retaining photocatalytic function. The key shift was not merely empirical color change or improved absorption: the paper argued, using first-principles calculations and x-ray photoemission evidence, that substitutional nitrogen narrowed the band gap in a chemically specific way and was indispensable to the observed visible-light activity.

This made visible-light-responsive oxide photocatalysis a credible materials-design program rather than a marginal modification of UV photocatalysis. After this work, researchers could pursue doped TiO₂ for solar and indoor-light applications such as pollutant degradation, self-cleaning surfaces, antimicrobial coatings, and photoinduced hydrophilicity under more realistic illumination. It also helped establish band-gap engineering of robust wide-band-gap oxides as a mainstream strategy: anion doping, co-doping, defect control, and heterostructure design became standard routes for trying to preserve TiO₂-like stability while harvesting a larger part of the solar spectrum.

The paper’s influence also lay in connecting computation, spectroscopy, and photocatalytic testing into a persuasive design loop. Later breakthroughs in visible-light photocatalysis, including nonmetal-doped oxides, nitrogen-modified photocatalysts, and more elaborate junction or sensitization schemes, built on the same premise that electronic structure could be deliberately reshaped to unlock new illumination regimes. Even where subsequent work exposed complications such as recombination centers, dopant stability, and variable activity across synthesis methods, the 2001 study remained a reference point because it made visible-light TiO₂ a tractable and widely pursued research frontier.

## Abstract

To use solar irradiation or interior lighting efficiently, we sought a photocatalyst with high reactivity under visible light. Films and powders of TiO 2- x N x have revealed an improvement over titanium dioxide (TiO 2 ) under visible light (wavelength &lt; 500 nanometers) in optical absorption and photocatalytic activity such as photodegradations of methylene blue and gaseous acetaldehyde and hydrophilicity of the film surface. Nitrogen doped into substitutional sites of TiO 2 has proven to be indispensable for band-gap narrowing and photocatalytic activity, as assessed by first-principles calculations and x-ray photoemission spectroscopy.
