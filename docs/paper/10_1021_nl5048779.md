# Nanocrystals of Cesium Lead Halide Perovskites (CsPbX<sub>3</sub>, X = Cl, Br, and I): Novel Optoelectronic Materials Showing Bright Emission with Wide Color Gamut

## Why this mattered

Before this paper, halide perovskites were known mainly as thin-film photovoltaic absorbers, not as a serious platform for colloidal quantum dots. Proteşescu *et al.* showed that fully inorganic CsPbX₃ nanocrystals could be synthesized as monodisperse, solution-processable nanocubes with size- and composition-tunable emission across the visible spectrum. The key shift was that perovskite nanocrystals combined traits that had rarely coexisted in one material system: narrow emission linewidths, high photoluminescence quantum yields, fast radiative lifetimes, inexpensive precursors, and facile halide-based color tuning.

This made a new class of optoelectronic materials practically imaginable. Instead of relying mainly on II-VI or III-V semiconductor quantum dots, whose synthesis, toxicity, surface chemistry, and blue/green stability posed persistent obstacles, researchers could use ionic lead-halide perovskites whose bandgaps were readily adjusted by halide composition and quantum confinement. The reported color gamut, reaching beyond standard display requirements, helped establish CsPbX₃ nanocrystals as candidates for displays, LEDs, lasers, scintillators, and other light-emitting technologies.

The paper mattered because it redirected a rapidly growing perovskite field from solar absorption toward high-performance emission. Subsequent work built directly on this platform: perovskite quantum-dot LEDs, amplified spontaneous emission and nanocrystal lasers, anion-exchange color tuning, surface-passivation strategies, and efforts to improve stability, encapsulation, and lead-management all trace part of their momentum to the materials space opened here. Its lasting significance is not that it solved the durability and toxicity problems of lead-halide perovskites, but that it made clear that these materials could be among the brightest and most spectrally tunable colloidal emitters known.

## Abstract

Metal halides perovskites, such as hybrid organic-inorganic CH3NH3PbI3, are newcomer optoelectronic materials that have attracted enormous attention as solution-deposited absorbing layers in solar cells with power conversion efficiencies reaching 20%. Herein we demonstrate a new avenue for halide perovskites by designing highly luminescent perovskite-based colloidal quantum dot materials. We have synthesized monodisperse colloidal nanocubes (4-15 nm edge lengths) of fully inorganic cesium lead halide perovskites (CsPbX3, X = Cl, Br, and I or mixed halide systems Cl/Br and Br/I) using inexpensive commercial precursors. Through compositional modulations and quantum size-effects, the bandgap energies and emission spectra are readily tunable over the entire visible spectral region of 410-700 nm. The photoluminescence of CsPbX3 nanocrystals is characterized by narrow emission line-widths of 12-42 nm, wide color gamut covering up to 140% of the NTSC color standard, high quantum yields of up to 90%, and radiative lifetimes in the range of 1-29 ns. The compelling combination of enhanced optical properties and chemical robustness makes CsPbX3 nanocrystals appealing for optoelectronic applications, particularly for blue and green spectral regions (410-530 nm), where typical metal chalcogenide-based quantum dots suffer from photodegradation.
