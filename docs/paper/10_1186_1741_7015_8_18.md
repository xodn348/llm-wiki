# CONSORT 2010 Statement: updated guidelines for reporting parallel group randomised trials

## Why this mattered

CONSORT 2010 mattered because it turned randomized-trial reporting from a largely narrative convention into a reproducible, auditable standard. By updating the earlier CONSORT statements with new methodological evidence and broad editorial experience, the paper made explicit what readers needed to judge a parallel-group randomized controlled trial: how participants were allocated, how outcomes were defined and analyzed, how harms and losses were reported, and where bias might enter. Its checklist and flow diagram did not change trial design directly, but they changed what counted as an adequately communicated trial.

The paradigm shift was cultural as much as technical. CONSORT 2010 helped make incomplete reporting a recognizable methodological failure rather than a mere writing problem. Once journals, reviewers, and trialists could point to a shared standard, systematic reviewers and guideline developers could more reliably extract comparable evidence across studies. That made later evidence-synthesis workflows, clinical-practice guidelines, and risk-of-bias assessment more dependable, because the primary reports increasingly contained the details needed to evaluate internal validity.

Its influence also extended beyond parallel-group trials. CONSORT 2010 became a model for reporting-guideline ecosystems across medicine, including extensions for cluster trials, noninferiority trials, abstracts, harms, pragmatic trials, and other designs. In that sense, the paper helped define a modern norm: high-impact clinical research is not complete when the experiment is finished, but when it is reported transparently enough for others to scrutinize, reproduce conceptually, synthesize, and use in patient-care decisions.

## Abstract

The CONSORT statement is used worldwide to improve the reporting of randomised controlled trials. Kenneth Schulz and colleagues describe the latest version, CONSORT 2010, which updates the reporting guideline based on new methodological evidence and accumulating experience.To encourage dissemination of the CONSORT 2010 Statement, this article is freely accessible on bmj.com and will also be published in the Lancet, Obstetrics and Gynecology, PLoS Medicine, Annals of Internal Medicine, Open Medicine, Journal of Clinical Epidemiology, BMC Medicine, and Trials.
