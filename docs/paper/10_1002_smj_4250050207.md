# A resource‐based view of the firm

## Why this mattered

Wernerfelt’s paper mattered because it helped shift strategic management from asking mainly *which product markets a firm should enter* to asking *what durable resources make particular strategies possible*. By introducing concepts such as “resource position barriers” and “resource-product matrices,” it reframed competitive advantage as something rooted not only in industry structure or market position, but also in firm-specific assets, capabilities, knowledge, and constraints. This made it possible to analyze diversification, entry, growth, and profitability through the accumulated resource base of the firm rather than treating the firm as a portfolio of products alone.

The paper did not immediately displace product-market strategy, but it supplied a conceptual vocabulary for what became the resource-based view of the firm. Later work, especially Jay Barney’s 1991 formulation of valuable, rare, imperfectly imitable, and non-substitutable resources, turned this perspective into one of the central theories of strategic management. It also helped prepare the ground for later breakthroughs on core competencies, dynamic capabilities, knowledge-based views of the firm, and capability-based explanations of persistent performance differences.

Its lasting importance is that it made internal heterogeneity theoretically central. Firms could now be studied not merely as competitors occupying positions in attractive industries, but as historically developed bundles of resources whose strategic options differ because their assets and capabilities differ. That change opened a path for explaining why firms in the same industry, facing similar external conditions, can achieve systematically different outcomes.

## Abstract

Abstract The paper explores the usefulness of analysing firms from the resource side rather than from the product side. In analogy to entry barriers and growth‐share matrices, the concepts of resource position barrier and resource‐product matrices are suggested. These tools are then used to highlight the new strategic options which naturally emerge from the resource perspective.
