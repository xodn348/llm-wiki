# Cleavage of GSDMD by inflammatory caspases determines pyroptotic cell death

## Why this mattered

Before this work, pyroptosis was defined largely upstream: inflammasomes activated inflammatory caspases, and those caspases promoted IL-1β/IL-18 maturation and lytic cell death. Shi and colleagues shifted the field by identifying gasdermin D as the missing executioner substrate. Their CRISPR screens showed that caspase-1- and caspase-11-dependent pyroptosis required GSDMD, and that inflammatory caspases cleaved it to generate the death-inducing fragment. Pyroptosis was no longer just “caspase-mediated inflammatory death”; it became a genetically and biochemically traceable pathway with a separable execution step.

That changed what was experimentally possible. Researchers could now distinguish inflammasome activation from membrane rupture, cytokine processing from cytokine release, and canonical caspase-1 pathways from noncanonical caspase-11/4/5 pathways while following a shared terminal effector. The paper also gave the field a concrete therapeutic target: blocking GSDMD cleavage or its pore-forming activity promised a way to dampen damaging inflammatory cell death without necessarily abolishing all upstream pathogen sensing.

Its influence is visible in the breakthroughs that followed. Subsequent work established the gasdermin N-terminal fragment as a pore-forming module, generalized the concept to other gasdermins such as GSDME, and connected pyroptotic execution to sepsis, autoinflammatory disease, infection, cancer therapy responses, and tissue injury. In retrospect, the paper helped convert pyroptosis from a phenomenological cell-death category into a molecular family of regulated membrane-permeabilizing events.
