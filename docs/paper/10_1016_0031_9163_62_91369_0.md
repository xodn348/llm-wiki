# Possible new effects in superconductive tunnelling

## Why this mattered

Josephson’s 1962 paper mattered because it showed that superconductivity could produce a genuinely new tunnelling phenomenon, not just ordinary single-particle tunnelling modified by an energy gap. From the BCS picture, Josephson predicted that two superconductors separated by a thin insulating barrier could carry a zero-voltage supercurrent, with the current determined by the phase difference between the superconducting wavefunctions. This made the macroscopic quantum phase of a superconductor experimentally consequential in a direct electrical device, turning what had been a theoretical order-parameter feature into an observable and controllable circuit variable.

The paper also predicted that a constant voltage across the junction would make the supercurrent oscillate at a frequency proportional to that voltage. This “AC Josephson effect” created an exact link between voltage and frequency through fundamental constants, which later became central to precision metrology and the modern voltage standard. More broadly, Josephson junctions made coherent quantum dynamics available in compact electrical circuits, enabling SQUID magnetometers, ultrasensitive detectors, superconducting digital logic, and eventually superconducting qubits.

Its paradigm shift was therefore twofold: it confirmed that quantum coherence could persist and be engineered at a macroscopic circuit scale, and it introduced a device element whose behavior depended on phase, not merely charge or resistance. Subsequent breakthroughs in quantum interference, precision measurement, and circuit quantum electrodynamics all depend on that move from superconductivity as a bulk state of matter to superconductivity as a phase-coherent technology.
