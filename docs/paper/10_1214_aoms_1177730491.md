# On a Test of Whether one of Two Random Variables is Stochastically Larger than the Other

## Why this mattered

Mann and Whitney’s paper helped turn rank-based inference from a practical suggestion into a mathematically grounded alternative to parametric testing. Wilcoxon had already proposed an equivalent procedure in 1945, but Mann and Whitney supplied the distributional machinery: a recurrence for the null distribution of the statistic, finite-sample tables, exact moments, an asymptotic normal limit, and a consistency result against ordered alternatives. That made it possible to test whether one population tends to yield larger observations than another without assuming normality, equal variances, or a particular parametric family.

The shift was methodological as much as technical. The paper showed that information in relative orderings alone could support rigorous hypothesis tests with known operating behavior. This mattered in fields where measurements were ordinal, skewed, contaminated by outliers, or too sparse for reliable distributional modeling. The resulting Mann–Whitney U test, also known through its equivalence to the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, became one of the canonical tools of nonparametric statistics because it offered a simple, reproducible way to compare two independent samples under weak assumptions.

Its influence also reaches into later statistical practice. The paper sits in the same postwar movement that produced modern distribution-free methods, permutation tests, rank tests, and robust inference. By proving exact and limiting properties for a statistic defined through ranks, Mann and Whitney helped establish a template for later breakthroughs: replace fragile model assumptions with invariance, ordering, and combinatorial probability, then recover usable large-sample approximations. That template remains visible in modern biomedical studies, psychology, ecology, machine learning evaluation, and any setting where two groups must be compared but the scale or distribution of the measurements cannot be taken for granted.

## Abstract

Let $x$ and $y$ be two random variables with continuous cumulative distribution functions $f$ and $g$. A statistic $U$ depending on the relative ranks of the $x$'s and $y$'s is proposed for testing the hypothesis $f = g$. Wilcoxon proposed an equivalent test in the Biometrics Bulletin, December, 1945, but gave only a few points of the distribution of his statistic. Under the hypothesis $f = g$ the probability of obtaining a given $U$ in a sample of $n x's$ and $m y's$ is the solution of a certain recurrence relation involving $n$ and $m$. Using this recurrence relation tables have been computed giving the probability of $U$ for samples up to $n = m = 8$. At this point the distribution is almost normal. From the recurrence relation explicit expressions for the mean, variance, and fourth moment are obtained. The 2rth moment is shown to have a certain form which enabled us to prove that the limit distribution is normal if $m, n$ go to infinity in any arbitrary manner. The test is shown to be consistent with respect to the class of alternatives $f(x) > g(x)$ for every $x$.
