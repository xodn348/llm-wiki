# Construction of Biologically Functional Bacterial Plasmids <i>In Vitro</i>

## Why this mattered

This paper showed that DNA could be deliberately cut, recombined, introduced into living cells, and maintained there as a functional genetic element. Before this, plasmids and viral genomes could be studied, transferred, or mutated, but Cohen, Chang, Boyer, and Helling demonstrated a general experimental route for constructing new replicons *in vitro* from defined DNA fragments and then recovering them as biologically active molecules in *E. coli*. The crucial shift was from observing genetic recombination as a natural cellular process to engineering it as a laboratory method.

What became newly possible was molecular cloning: specific DNA segments could be joined to plasmid vectors, propagated in bacteria, and selected by their genetic markers. This made genes portable, amplifiable, and experimentally tractable in a way that earlier microbial genetics did not. The paper therefore helped establish recombinant DNA technology as a practical platform rather than a conceptual possibility, linking restriction enzymes, ligation, transformation, and plasmid replication into a reproducible workflow.

Its consequences were broad. The ability to clone and propagate defined DNA sequences underwrote gene mapping, sequencing, expression studies, and later biotechnology. It was a necessary precursor to recombinant insulin and other therapeutic proteins, engineered microbes, transgenic organisms, DNA libraries, and much of modern genomics. The work also helped trigger the early recombinant DNA safety debates, because it made clear that novel genetic combinations were no longer only products of nature or breeding, but could be constructed intentionally and propagated in living cells.

## Abstract

The construction of new plasmid DNA species by in vitro joining of restriction endonuclease-generated fragments of separate plasmids is described. Newly constructed plasmids that are inserted into Escherichia coli by transformation are shown to be biologically functional replicons that possess genetic properties and nucleotide base sequences from both of the parent DNA molecules. Functional plasmids can be obtained by reassociation of endonuclease-generated fragments of larger replicons, as well as by joining of plasmid DNA molecules of entirely different origins.
