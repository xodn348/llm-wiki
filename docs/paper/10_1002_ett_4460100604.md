# Capacity of Multi‐antenna Gaussian Channels

## Why this mattered

Telatar’s paper helped turn multi-antenna communication from an engineering trick into an information-theoretic paradigm. Before this line of work, extra antennas were often understood mainly as a way to improve reliability through diversity or to shape energy through beamforming. Telatar showed, in a precise Gaussian-channel capacity framework, that independent transmit-receive antenna paths could create multiple parallel spatial dimensions. Under the paper’s independence assumptions for fading and receiver noise, capacity could grow dramatically with the number of antennas, making spectrum reuse in space a fundamental resource rather than a marginal hardware improvement.

What became newly possible was a rigorous benchmark for MIMO systems: capacities, error exponents, and computational procedures for channels with and without fading. This gave later researchers and system designers a way to ask not merely whether a multi-antenna scheme worked, but how close it came to the channel’s theoretical limits. The paper’s log-determinant/eigenvalue view of the channel also tied wireless communication to random matrix methods, helping make spatial multiplexing, outage capacity analysis, and antenna-correlation modeling central tools in the field.

Its influence is visible in the breakthroughs that followed: space-time coding, BLAST-style layered architectures, MIMO-OFDM, multiuser MIMO, and eventually the antenna-rich designs used in 4G, 5G, and massive MIMO. Telatar did not by itself supply all of those engineering systems, but it clarified why they were worth pursuing: multiple antennas could multiply throughput without requiring proportional new bandwidth or transmit power. That reframed wireless capacity as a problem of exploiting channel geometry, not just fighting noise.

## Abstract

Abstract We investigate the use of multiple transmitting and/or receiving antennas for single user communications over the additive Gaussian channel with and without fading. We derive formulas for the capacities and error exponents of such channels, and describe computational procedures to evaluate such formulas. We show that the potential gains of such multi‐antenna systems over single‐antenna systems is rather large under independenceassumptions for the fades and noises at different receiving antennas.
