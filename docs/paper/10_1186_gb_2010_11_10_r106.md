# Differential expression analysis for sequence count data

## Why this mattered

Before Anders and Huber, RNA-seq was often treated as a more quantitative successor to microarrays, but the statistical infrastructure for sequencing counts was still immature. This paper helped establish that read counts should be modeled as discrete, overdispersed data rather than as approximately continuous measurements or simple Poisson observations. Its central move was to use the negative binomial distribution, with the mean-variance relationship estimated across genes, so that biological variability could be separated from sampling noise even with the small replicate numbers typical of early RNA-seq experiments.

That shift made differential expression analysis substantially more reliable and accessible. DESeq gave biologists a practical Bioconductor implementation for testing gene-level changes while accounting for library size, dynamic range, and gene-specific variability. In doing so, it helped turn RNA-seq from a promising measurement technology into a routine inferential tool: researchers could move beyond counting reads and begin making statistically controlled claims about transcriptional regulation across conditions.

Its influence is visible in the next generation of genomics methods. Later tools, including DESeq2 and edgeR-centered workflows, refined dispersion estimation, normalization, shrinkage, and generalized linear modeling, but retained the core paradigm that sequencing-based differential analysis requires count-specific probabilistic models. This framework became foundational not only for bulk RNA-seq, but also for many later count-based assays in functional genomics, where the key problem is still the same: infer biological signal from sparse, uneven, overdispersed sequencing counts.

## Abstract

High-throughput sequencing assays such as RNA-Seq, ChIP-Seq or barcode counting provide quantitative readouts in the form of count data. To infer differential signal in such data correctly and with good statistical power, estimation of data variability throughout the dynamic range and a suitable error model are required. We propose a method based on the negative binomial distribution, with variance and mean linked by local regression and present an implementation, DESeq, as an R/Bioconductor package.
