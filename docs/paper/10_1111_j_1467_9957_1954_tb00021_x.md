# Economic Development with Unlimited Supplies of Labour

## Why this mattered

Lewis’s 1954 paper mattered because it gave development economics a formal organizing model for economies in which a small capitalist sector coexisted with a large subsistence sector. Instead of treating “labour scarcity” as the universal constraint, Lewis argued that many poor economies had surplus labour in traditional agriculture whose marginal product was very low, so industrial expansion could proceed for a time without sharply raising wages. This reframed development as a structural transformation problem: growth depended on mobilizing savings and investment in the capitalist sector, drawing workers out of subsistence activity, and reinvesting profits until the surplus-labour pool was exhausted.

The paradigm shift was that poverty and growth could now be analyzed through sectoral dynamics rather than only through aggregate capital accumulation or comparative-static trade theory. The paper made it newly possible to ask when industrialization would absorb underemployed rural labour, why wages might remain flat during early growth, why profit reinvestment mattered, and why the transition point at which labour became scarce would change the distributional and growth dynamics of an economy. Its “dual-sector” model became a baseline for later work on structural change, rural-urban migration, and development planning.

Subsequent breakthroughs often refined, challenged, or generalized Lewis’s framework. Ranis and Fei extended the dual-economy model; Harris and Todaro complicated the migration mechanism by introducing expected urban wages and unemployment; later empirical work on East Asian industrialization, demographic transition, and sectoral productivity gaps repeatedly returned to Lewis’s central question: how economies move labour from low-productivity to high-productivity uses. Even where its assumptions proved too simple, the paper established the vocabulary and analytical frame for studying development as transformation, not merely expansion.
