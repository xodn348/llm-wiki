# Quantum cryptography based on Bell’s theorem

## Why this mattered

Ekert’s 1991 paper shifted quantum cryptography from a protocol based mainly on nonorthogonal quantum states, as in BB84, to one grounded directly in entanglement and Bell nonlocality. Its key conceptual move was to make the violation of a Bell inequality not merely a foundational test of quantum mechanics, but an operational security test: correlations strong enough to violate a Bell bound certify that an eavesdropper has not acquired the shared key without disturbing the quantum system. This tied cryptographic secrecy to one of the deepest empirical departures of quantum theory from classical physics.

The paper made entanglement a practical cryptographic resource. By proposing key distribution using pairs of spin-1/2 particles in the singlet state, with security checked through Bell-type correlations, Ekert showed that the same phenomenon behind the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen debate could be used as an information-processing primitive. After this, quantum cryptography was no longer only about the impossibility of measuring unknown quantum states without disturbance; it was also about certifying private randomness and correlations through observable violations of classical limits.

Its influence reached well beyond the specific E91 protocol. The paper helped establish entanglement-based quantum information as a central paradigm, feeding into later work on device-independent quantum key distribution, randomness certification, and security proofs based on monogamy of entanglement and nonlocal correlations. In retrospect, it connected three fields that had often been treated separately: quantum foundations, cryptographic security, and experimental quantum communication.

## Abstract

Practical application of the generalized Bell's theorem in the so-called key distribution process in cryptography is reported. The proposed scheme is based on the Bohm's version of the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen gedanken experiment and Bell's theorem is used to test for eavesdropping.
