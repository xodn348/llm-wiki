# THE ELECTRODYNAMICS OF SUBSTANCES WITH SIMULTANEOUSLY NEGATIVE VALUES OF $\epsilon$ AND μ

## Why this mattered

Veselago’s 1968 paper mattered because it showed that Maxwell’s equations allow a physically coherent class of media in which both permittivity \(\epsilon\) and permeability \(\mu\) are negative over the same frequency range. Before this, optical and electromagnetic intuition was built almost entirely around positive-index media. Veselago demonstrated that the simultaneous sign reversal does not simply make waves impossible; instead it produces a negative refractive index, with the wave vector, Poynting vector, and energy flow arranged in a counterintuitive “left-handed” geometry. This reframed refraction, Doppler shift, Cherenkov radiation, and radiation pressure as effects whose familiar signs depended on material response, not on unavoidable laws of propagation.

The paper’s immediate impact was conceptual rather than technological, because no natural material with the required simultaneous negative \(\epsilon\) and \(\mu\) was known. Its importance became clear decades later when engineered metamaterials made Veselago’s medium experimentally accessible. Work in the late 1990s and early 2000s on artificial resonant structures, negative-index composites, and microwave demonstrations turned the paper from a theoretical possibility into a research program. Veselago’s analysis supplied the language and predictions that made those results intelligible.

This shift opened paths to phenomena that had previously seemed exotic or impossible in ordinary optics: negative refraction, reversed phase propagation, unusual lensing behavior, and new control over electromagnetic wave trajectories. It also provided the conceptual foundation for later breakthroughs in metamaterials, including superlensing proposals, transformation optics, cloaking research, and broader efforts to design materials by specifying electromagnetic response rather than accepting the responses found in nature. Its lasting significance is that it changed “material properties” from passive inputs to design variables in wave physics.
