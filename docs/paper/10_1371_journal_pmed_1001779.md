# UK Biobank: An Open Access Resource for Identifying the Causes of a Wide Range of Complex Diseases of Middle and Old Age

## Why this mattered

Before UK Biobank, many studies of complex disease were either deep but small, or large but narrow. Sudlow et al. made the case for a different research instrument: a prospective, population-scale cohort of about 500,000 middle-aged participants, with baseline lifestyle, environmental, physical, cognitive, biological-sample, genetic, and linked health-record data made available through a non-preferential access system. The shift was not a single hypothesis about one disease, but an infrastructure claim: that the causes of common disorders could be studied more powerfully if many exposures, genotypes, intermediate traits, and later clinical outcomes were measured in the same people before disease onset.

That design changed what became routinely possible. Researchers could move from one-exposure/one-outcome epidemiology toward phenome-wide and genome-wide analyses across thousands of traits, ask whether risk factors were shared across diseases, and use genetics as a tool for causal inference through Mendelian randomization. The paper also helped normalize open-access cohort science at national scale: UK Biobank was presented not as a private dataset for its founders, but as a public research platform whose value would grow as follow-up, assays, imaging, and disease events accumulated.

Its influence is visible in later breakthroughs built directly on this architecture: large-scale GWAS using UK Biobank genetics, imaging-genetics studies linking variants to brain and body phenotypes, exome and proteomic analyses, polygenic-risk work, and cross-disease maps of genetic risk. These advances did not make UK Biobank free of limitations, including healthy-volunteer bias and ancestry imbalance, but they did establish a new paradigm for biomedicine: build reusable, deeply phenotyped, consented population resources first, then let many independent investigators discover disease mechanisms, biomarkers, and therapeutic leads over time.

## Abstract

Cathie Sudlow and colleagues describe the UK Biobank, a large population-based prospective study, established to allow investigation of the genetic and non-genetic determinants of the diseases of middle and old age.
