# <i>C</i><i>P</i>-Violation in the Renormalizable Theory of Weak Interaction

## Why this mattered

Kobayashi and Maskawa turned CP violation from an isolated puzzle of neutral kaon physics into a structural clue about the weak interaction. Working within the newly renormalizable electroweak framework, they showed that the then-standard four-quark “quartet” scheme could not naturally accommodate CP violation without adding new ingredients. Their decisive step was to identify that a complex phase capable of producing CP violation appears in the quark mixing matrix only when there are at least three generations of quarks. In 1973, only three quarks were established, and charm was still not experimentally confirmed; the paper therefore implied not just a mechanism, but an enlarged matter spectrum.

This changed what CP violation could mean theoretically. Rather than requiring an ad hoc superweak force or explicit new CP-violating fields, it could arise from the same charged-current weak interactions already responsible for flavor change, once quark mixing had the right dimensionality. The result made a six-quark world newly plausible: alongside the known up, down, and strange quarks, it pointed toward charm, bottom, and top as part of a coherent electroweak flavor structure. After the discoveries of charm, bottom, and top, the Kobayashi-Maskawa mechanism became the Standard Model account of quark-sector CP violation.

Its later impact was especially clear in B-meson physics. The CKM matrix, as the Kobayashi-Maskawa mixing framework came to be known, predicted a rich pattern of CP-violating effects beyond kaons, allowing precision tests at experiments such as BaBar, Belle, and later LHCb. The observation of large CP violation in B decays confirmed that the mechanism was not merely a way to fit a historical anomaly, but a predictive organizing principle for flavor physics. At the same time, its insufficiency to explain the cosmic matter-antimatter asymmetry helped define one of the central open problems beyond the Standard Model.

## Abstract

In a framework of the renormalizable theory of weak interaction, problems of CP-violation are studied. It is concluded that no realistic models of CP-violation exist in the quartet scheme without introducing any other new fields. Some possible models of CP-violation are also discussed.
