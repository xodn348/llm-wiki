# Note on an Approximation Treatment for Many-Electron Systems

## Why this mattered

Møller and Plesset’s paper mattered because it turned Hartree–Fock from an endpoint into a starting point. Hartree–Fock supplied a tractable independent-particle picture, but it left out electron correlation: the systematic lowering of the energy that comes from electrons avoiding one another beyond the mean field. By formulating perturbation theory with the Hartree–Fock solution as the zero-order approximation, Møller and Plesset gave quantum chemists a controlled way to compute correlation corrections while retaining the molecular-orbital framework that was already becoming central to electronic-structure theory.

The key shift was practical as well as conceptual. The paper showed that the first-order correction to the energy vanishes and that the second-order correction simplifies sharply because of the special structure of the Hartree–Fock reference. This made what is now called MP2 a comparatively economical post-Hartree–Fock method: more accurate than mean-field theory, but far less demanding than a full treatment of the many-electron wavefunction. After this, electron correlation could be treated not only as a qualitative deficiency of Hartree–Fock, but as a computable hierarchy of corrections derived from a definite one-electron problem.

Its influence runs through much of later quantum chemistry. Møller–Plesset perturbation theory became one of the standard benchmarks and workhorses for molecular energies, conformational comparisons, noncovalent interactions, and reaction chemistry, especially in the decades when computational cost strongly constrained method choice. It also helped establish the broader paradigm of post-Hartree–Fock electronic-structure methods: start from a single-particle reference, then add correlation systematically. Later configuration-interaction, coupled-cluster, many-body perturbation, and composite thermochemical methods developed in dialogue with this same problem, making the 1934 paper a foundation for the modern accuracy hierarchy of ab initio quantum chemistry.

## Abstract

A perturbation theory is developed for treating a system of $n$ electrons in which the Hartree-Fock solution appears as the zero-order approximation. It is shown by this development that the first order correction for the energy and the charge density of the system is zero. The expression for the second-order correction for the energy greatly simplifies because of the special property of the zero-order solution. It is pointed out that the development of the higher approximation involves only calculations based on a definite one-body problem.
