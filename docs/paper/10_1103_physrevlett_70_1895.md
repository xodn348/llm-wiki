# Teleporting an unknown quantum state via dual classical and Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen channels

## Why this mattered

Bennett, Brassard, Crépeau, Jozsa, Peres, and Wootters showed that an unknown quantum state could be transferred without being measured, copied, or physically carried from sender to receiver. The paper reframed entanglement from a philosophical puzzle about EPR correlations into an operational resource: if Alice and Bob shared an entangled pair in advance, then two classical bits plus that shared nonclassical correlation were sufficient to reconstruct an arbitrary qubit at Bob’s location. The result was striking because it respected the no-cloning theorem and ordinary causality while still making possible a task that had no classical analogue.

The paradigm shift was that quantum information became separable from the material system that initially embodied it. Alice’s measurement destroyed the original state, while Bob’s conditional operation recreated it elsewhere; what moved was not a particle but the state itself, mediated jointly by classical communication and entanglement. This gave precise information-theoretic meaning to entanglement and helped establish it as a quantifiable, consumable resource alongside classical communication.

After this paper, teleportation became a central primitive of quantum information science. It underlies entanglement swapping, quantum repeaters, measurement-based quantum computation, fault-tolerant gate constructions, and networked quantum communication. Later experimental demonstrations turned the protocol into a benchmark for controlling entanglement across photons, ions, atoms, solid-state systems, and long-distance links. In that sense, the paper did not merely propose a surprising protocol; it helped define the architecture of quantum technologies built from shared entanglement, local operations, and classical communication.

## Abstract

An unknown quantum state \ensuremath{\Vert}\ensuremath{\varphi}〉 can be disassembled into, then later reconstructed from, purely classical information and purely nonclassical Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) correlations. To do so the sender, ``Alice,'' and the receiver, ``Bob,'' must prearrange the sharing of an EPR-correlated pair of particles. Alice makes a joint measurement on her EPR particle and the unknown quantum system, and sends Bob the classical result of this measurement. Knowing this, Bob can convert the state of his EPR particle into an exact replica of the unknown state \ensuremath{\Vert}\ensuremath{\varphi}〉 which Alice destroyed.
