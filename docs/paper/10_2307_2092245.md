# Studies in Ethnomethodology.

## Why this mattered

Garfinkel’s *Studies in Ethnomethodology* mattered because it shifted sociology’s object of explanation from social order as an external structure to social order as an ongoing, locally produced achievement. Its central move was to treat ordinary “common sense” reasoning not as noise beneath scientific analysis, but as a topic in its own right: the practical methods by which people make actions, identities, records, rules, and institutions intelligible to one another. That reframed everyday life as a rigorous empirical domain, challenging mid-century structural functionalism and survey-centered positivism without simply abandoning sociology’s concern with order.

After this work, researchers could study routine interaction, bureaucratic records, juror deliberation, clinical selection, gender status, and scientific reasoning as situated accomplishments rather than as mere expressions of hidden norms or attitudes. Concepts such as indexicality, reflexivity, the documentary method, and “members’ methods” made it possible to analyze how meaning depends on context while still being publicly recognizable and accountable. The famous studies of “Agnes,” jurors, clinic files, and breaching experiments showed that seemingly stable facts of social life are maintained through disciplined, observable practices.

Its downstream importance lies especially in conversation analysis, workplace studies, science and technology studies, medical sociology, and later interactional approaches to gender and identity. Sacks, Schegloff, Jefferson, and others extended Garfinkel’s program into fine-grained analysis of talk-in-interaction; later studies of laboratories, organizations, and professional work used similar premises to examine how facts, decisions, and institutional realities are produced in practice. The paradigm shift was not that social structures disappeared, but that their durability became something to be investigated in the concrete methods through which people continually make them real.
