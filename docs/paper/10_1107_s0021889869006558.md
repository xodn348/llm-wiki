# A profile refinement method for nuclear and magnetic structures

## Why this mattered

Rietveld’s 1969 paper changed powder diffraction from a method that mainly supplied extracted peak intensities into a full-pattern refinement technique. Instead of trying to separate and integrate individual reflections, which is especially difficult when powder peaks overlap, the method fitted the measured step-scan profile directly by least squares. That was the conceptual shift: the entire diffraction pattern became the object of refinement, so overlapping peaks were no longer merely a limitation to be avoided but information that could be modeled.

This made accurate structural refinement possible for materials that could not readily be studied as single crystals, including many ceramics, minerals, magnetic solids, and later complex functional materials. The paper also showed that nuclear and magnetic structures could be treated within the same profile-refinement framework, provided the magnetic cell was commensurate with the nuclear cell. In practice, this opened a route from powder diffraction data to atomic positions, occupancies, displacement parameters, magnetic moments, and constrained structural models with a precision that had previously been associated much more strongly with single-crystal methods.

Its later importance came from becoming the foundation of the “Rietveld method,” now central to neutron, X-ray, and synchrotron powder diffraction. Subsequent advances in instrumentation, computing, profile functions, background modeling, preferred-orientation correction, multiphase analysis, and combined refinements all built on the same basic idea: refine a physically meaningful model against the whole observed pattern. That framework helped make powder diffraction a routine tool for solving and validating structures in solid-state chemistry, materials science, geoscience, and magnetism, rather than only indexing phases or measuring lattice constants.

## Abstract

A structure refinement method is described which does not use integrated neutron powder intensities, single or overlapping, but employs directly the profile intensities obtained from step-scanning measurements of the powder diagram. Nuclear as well as magnetic structures can be refined, the latter only when their magnetic unit cell is equal to, or a multiple of, the nuclear cell. The least-squares refinement procedure allows, with a simple code, the introduction of linear or quadratic constraints between the parameters.
