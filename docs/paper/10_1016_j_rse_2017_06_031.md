# Google Earth Engine: Planetary-scale geospatial analysis for everyone

## Why this mattered

Before Earth Engine, planetary-scale remote sensing was possible mainly for groups that could acquire huge archives, manage storage, build processing pipelines, and run substantial compute infrastructure. Gorelick et al. mattered because it reframed geospatial analysis from a data-download problem into a cloud-native, analysis-ready platform problem. The paper described an integrated environment where global satellite archives, scalable computation, and shareable code lived together, allowing researchers and practitioners to ask questions over decades of imagery without first becoming infrastructure engineers.

The paradigm shift was democratization at planetary scale. Earth Engine made it newly practical to produce repeatable global or regional analyses of forest loss, surface water change, crop conditions, urban expansion, drought, fires, and disaster impacts with methods that could be shared, inspected, and rerun. That changed the tempo of remote sensing: analyses that once required bespoke pipelines and long preprocessing stages could become interactive prototypes, public apps, or operational monitoring systems.

Its influence is visible in later breakthroughs that treat Earth observation as a continuously queryable public resource rather than a collection of files. Large-scale products such as global forest-change maps, dynamic surface-water datasets, land-cover monitoring, and climate-impact assessments benefited from the same premise: put the data near the computation, expose high-level abstractions, and let domain experts work at geographic and temporal scales that match the problem. In that sense, the paper helped define the modern expectation that environmental science, humanitarian response, and policy-relevant geospatial monitoring should be global, reproducible, and accessible beyond specialist supercomputing centers.

## Abstract

Google Earth Engine is a cloud-based platform for planetary-scale geospatial analysis that brings Google's massive computational capabilities to bear on a variety of high-impact societal issues including deforestation, drought, disaster, disease, food security, water management, climate monitoring and environmental protection. It is unique in the field as an integrated platform designed to empower not only traditional remote sensing scientists, but also a much wider audience that lacks the technical capacity needed to utilize traditional supercomputers or large-scale commodity cloud computing resources.
