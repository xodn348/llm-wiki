# Two‐Dimensional Nanocrystals Produced by Exfoliation of Ti<sub>3</sub>AlC<sub>2</sub>

## Why this mattered

This paper mattered because it showed that atomically thin materials were not limited to weakly bonded layered solids like graphite. By selectively removing aluminum from the MAX phase Ti<sub>3</sub>AlC<sub>2</sub>, Naguib and colleagues produced two-dimensional Ti<sub>3</sub>C<sub>2</sub> sheets, multilayers, and scrolls at room temperature. The key conceptual shift was that a strongly bonded ceramic could be converted into a new 2D material by chemical exfoliation, creating a route from layered ternary carbides and nitrides to an entirely new materials family later known as MXenes.

What became newly possible was a programmable path to conductive, chemically tunable 2D transition-metal carbides and nitrides. Because Ti<sub>3</sub>AlC<sub>2</sub> was only one member of a much larger MAX-phase family, the paper immediately implied that many related 2D compositions could be synthesized by choosing different M, A, and X elements. It also introduced a surface-terminated 2D material platform, since etching in HF produced Ti<sub>3</sub>C<sub>2</sub> surfaces decorated with functional groups such as fluorine, oxygen, and hydroxyl species, making chemistry, interlayer spacing, and properties adjustable in ways not available for graphene.

Subsequent breakthroughs followed from that opening: MXenes became a broad research field spanning electrochemical energy storage, electromagnetic interference shielding, water treatment membranes, sensors, catalysis, and printable conductive inks. The 2011 paper did not solve all of those applications, but it changed the search space. Instead of asking whether graphene-like behavior could be reproduced in a few known layered crystals, researchers could explore a large, compositionally diverse family of metallic or semimetallic 2D ceramics with tunable surfaces and interlayers.

## Abstract

2D Ti3C2 nanosheets, multilayer structures, and conical scrolls produced by room temperature exfoliation of Ti3AlC2 in HF are reported. Since Ti3AlC2 is a member of a 60+ group of layered ternary carbides and nitrides, this discovery opens a door to the synthesis of a large number of other 2D crystals.
