# Two-Dimensional Magnetotransport in the Extreme Quantum Limit

## Why this mattered

Tsui, Störmer, and Gossard’s 1982 observation showed that the quantum Hall effect was not limited to the integer filling of single-particle Landau levels. A Hall plateau at \(\rho_{xy}=3h/e^2\), paired with a minimum in \(\rho_{xx}\), appeared when the lowest spin-polarized Landau level was only one-third filled. That was a direct experimental sign that electron-electron interactions could produce a new incompressible quantum state with precisely quantized transport, beyond the independent-electron picture that explained the integer quantum Hall effect.

The paradigm shift was that a two-dimensional electron gas in a strong magnetic field became a laboratory for emergent many-body phases, not just for measuring band structure or disorder-broadened Landau levels. The authors initially suggested a Wigner solid or charge-density-wave-like ordering as a possible explanation, reflecting how unexpected the result was. Within a year, Laughlin’s wavefunction reinterpreted the \(\nu=1/3\) state as a new correlated quantum liquid with fractionally charged excitations, turning the anomaly into the foundation of the fractional quantum Hall effect.

After this paper, magnetotransport could reveal topological order, quasiparticles with fractional charge, and eventually anyonic statistics in clean semiconductor heterostructures. It helped establish high-mobility GaAs/AlGaAs two-dimensional electron systems as precision platforms for strongly correlated quantum matter, leading to the hierarchy and composite-fermion pictures, broader studies of fractional filling factors, and the 1998 Nobel Prize awarded to Tsui, Störmer, and Laughlin.

## Abstract

A quantized Hall plateau of ${\ensuremath{\rho}}_{\mathrm{xy}}=\frac{3h}{{e}^{2}}$, accompanied by a minimum in ${\ensuremath{\rho}}_{\mathrm{xx}}$, was observed at $T&lt;5$ K in magnetotransport of high-mobility, two-dimensional electrons, when the lowest-energy, spin-polarized Landau level is $\frac{1}{3}$ filled. The formation of a Wigner solid or charge-density-wave state with triangular symmetry is suggested as a possible explanation.
