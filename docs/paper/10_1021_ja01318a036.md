# The Determination of Enzyme Dissociation Constants

## Why this mattered

Lineweaver and Burk’s paper mattered because it turned Michaelis-Menten enzyme kinetics into a practical graphical method for estimating constants from experimental data. By taking reciprocals of the Michaelis-Menten equation, they made it possible to plot `1/v` against `1/[S]`, converting a curved saturation relationship into a straight line whose intercepts could be used to estimate `Vmax` and the Michaelis constant. In the context of 1930s biochemistry, this was a major methodological shift: enzyme behavior could be compared quantitatively across preparations, substrates, inhibitors, and experimental conditions using a simple linear plot rather than relying only on qualitative activity curves.

The paper also helped establish enzyme kinetics as a discipline of parameter estimation. Once `Km`, `Vmax`, and related dissociation or inhibition constants could be extracted in a standardized way, enzymes became experimentally legible as molecular systems with measurable affinities and catalytic capacities. That made it easier to distinguish competitive, noncompetitive, and related modes of inhibition, and it gave biochemists a common visual language for reasoning about mechanism. The Lineweaver-Burk plot later became a standard teaching and laboratory tool, even though modern nonlinear regression is usually preferred because reciprocal plots overweight low-substrate data and can distort experimental error.

Its deeper influence was that it linked biochemical mechanism to quantitative model fitting. Subsequent breakthroughs in enzymology, metabolic regulation, drug inhibition, and molecular pharmacology depended on being able to infer constants from rate measurements and compare them across systems. The specific double-reciprocal plot is now often treated as a historical method rather than the best statistical practice, but the paper’s paradigm-shifting contribution endured: enzyme reactions could be represented by simple mathematical parameters, and those parameters could guide mechanistic interpretation and experimental design.
