# <scp>CHARMM</scp>: A program for macromolecular energy, minimization, and dynamics calculations

## Why this mattered

CHARMM mattered because it helped turn macromolecular modeling from a collection of specialized calculations into an integrated computational framework. Earlier molecular mechanics and dynamics methods existed, but CHARMM combined structure building, empirical energy evaluation, minimization, normal-mode analysis, molecular dynamics, and trajectory analysis in a flexible program aimed at proteins, nucleic acids, and other large biological molecules. That integration made it newly practical to ask mechanistic questions about macromolecules in atomic detail: not only what a structure looked like, but how its conformations, motions, and energetics were related.

The paradigm shift was methodological as much as technical. CHARMM embodied the idea that biological function could be studied through transferable empirical force fields and explicit simulation protocols, not only through static crystallographic models or qualitative physical intuition. By publishing the program’s capabilities, parameter set, and sample run, Brooks and colleagues made a reproducible computational style available to a wider community. The paper helped establish molecular dynamics and energy-based modeling as routine tools for structural biology, enzymology, protein folding studies, ligand binding, and conformational analysis.

Subsequent breakthroughs in biomolecular simulation built directly on this foundation: improved CHARMM force fields, longer-timescale protein dynamics, free-energy calculations, membrane and nucleic-acid simulations, and hybrid quantum/classical studies all depended on the same core premise that macromolecular behavior could be explored through programmable, parameterized physical models. CHARMM also influenced the broader ecosystem of simulation packages and force-field development. Its importance was not that it solved biological complexity in 1983, but that it made a durable computational paradigm usable: macromolecules could be treated as dynamic physical systems whose motions and energetics were calculable, testable, and progressively refinable.

## Abstract

Abstract CHARMM ( C hemistry at HAR vard M acromolecular M echanics) is a highly flexible computer program which uses empirical energy functions to model macromolecular systems. The program can read or model build structures, energy minimize them by first‐ or second‐derivative techniques, perform a normal mode or molecular dynamics simulation, and analyze the structural, equilibrium, and dynamic properties determined in these calculations. The operations that CHARMM can perform are described, and some implementation details are given. A set of parameters for the empirical energy function and a sample run are included.
