# Multilineage Potential of Adult Human Mesenchymal Stem Cells

## Why this mattered

Pittenger et al. mattered because it gave adult human marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells a concrete experimental identity at a time when stem-cell biology was strongly shaped by hematopoietic and embryonic models. The paper did not merely assert that marrow contained supportive stromal progenitors; it showed that cells isolated from adult human marrow could be expanded in vitro with a stable phenotype and then directed into osteogenic, chondrogenic, and adipogenic lineages. Most importantly, the authors traced multilineage capacity to individual colony-forming cells, strengthening the claim that multipotency was an intrinsic property of clonally expanded cells rather than a mixture of committed precursors.

That shifted what was experimentally and clinically imaginable. After this paper, adult human mesenchymal stem cells became a tractable platform: researchers could isolate them from donors, grow them as adherent monolayers, test lineage induction under defined culture conditions, and ask whether adult autologous cells could be used for skeletal repair, cartilage regeneration, marrow stromal support, or broader tissue-engineering strategies. The work helped move “adult stem cells” from a largely anatomical or supportive concept into a reproducible cell-biological system that could be standardized, compared across laboratories, and eventually manufactured.

Its influence also lies in the questions it made unavoidable. Later work complicated the early therapeutic optimism, showing that many effects attributed to mesenchymal stem cells in vivo are paracrine, immunomodulatory, or context-dependent rather than straightforward engraftment and tissue replacement. Even so, Pittenger et al. provided the reference point for the modern MSC field: the operational definition of culture-expanded human marrow MSCs, the tri-lineage differentiation assay, and the idea that adult somatic tissues could contain expandable progenitors with clinically relevant plasticity. Subsequent advances in regenerative medicine, cell therapy manufacturing, stromal immunobiology, and induced pluripotent stem-cell comparisons all inherited that framework.

## Abstract

Human mesenchymal stem cells are thought to be multipotent cells, which are present in adult marrow, that can replicate as undifferentiated cells and that have the potential to differentiate to lineages of mesenchymal tissues, including bone, cartilage, fat, tendon, muscle, and marrow stroma. Cells that have the characteristics of human mesenchymal stem cells were isolated from marrow aspirates of volunteer donors. These cells displayed a stable phenotype and remained as a monolayer in vitro. These adult stem cells could be induced to differentiate exclusively into the adipocytic, chondrocytic, or osteocytic lineages. Individual stem cells were identified that, when expanded to colonies, retained their multilineage potential.
