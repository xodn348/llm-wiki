# Reliable Perturbative Results for Strong Interactions?

## Why this mattered

Politzer’s paper helped overturn the expectation that strong interactions were intrinsically beyond controlled perturbation theory. By explicitly calculating the short-distance behavior of Yang-Mills theories, it showed that non-Abelian gauge couplings can decrease at high momentum: the property later called **asymptotic freedom**. This made it plausible that a theory could be strongly coupled at ordinary hadronic distances yet become calculable in deep Euclidean, high-energy regimes. That was the conceptual bridge needed to connect gauge theory with the observed near-scaling behavior in deep inelastic scattering.

The immediate consequence was that perturbation theory became a reliable tool for parts of the strong interaction, not because the force was weak everywhere, but because it became weak at short distances. Together with the contemporaneous work of Gross and Wilczek, Politzer’s result made quantum chromodynamics the compelling candidate theory of the strong force: quarks interacted through an SU(3) Yang-Mills gauge field, with high-energy processes accessible to calculation and low-energy confinement left as the complementary nonperturbative problem.

After this paper, strong-interaction physics could develop along two linked tracks: precision perturbative QCD for short-distance phenomena, and nonperturbative methods for hadrons, confinement, and chiral dynamics. The result underwrote later breakthroughs including quantitative scaling violations, parton evolution, jet physics, and the broader Standard Model picture in which the strong force is a renormalizable non-Abelian gauge theory. Its paradigm shift was not merely a new calculation, but a reversal of strategy: the apparent strength of the strong interaction no longer disqualified gauge theory; it became part of the theory’s explanation.

## Abstract

An explicit calculation shows perturbation theory to be arbitrarily good for the deep Euclidean Green's functions of any Yang-Mills theory and of many Yang-Mills theories with fermions. Under the hypothesis that spontaneous symmetry breakdown is of dynamical origin, these symmetric Green's functions are the asymptotic forms of the physically significant spontaneously broken solution, whose coupling could be strong.
