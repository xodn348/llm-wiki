# Quantized Hall Conductance in a Two-Dimensional Periodic Potential

## Why this mattered

This paper reframed the integer quantum Hall effect from a problem of microscopic material details into a topological statement about quantum states. Thouless, Kohmoto, Nightingale, and den Nijs showed that when the Fermi energy lies in an energy gap, the Hall conductance computed from the Kubo formula is quantized as an integer invariant of the occupied electronic bands. This integer, later called the TKNN invariant, made the extraordinary experimental precision of Hall plateaus intelligible without relying on disorder-specific or sample-specific explanations.

The conceptual shift was that electrical conductance could be protected by global geometry in quantum-mechanical parameter space. After this paper, band theory was no longer only a way to classify spectra and Fermi surfaces; it also became a way to classify phases by topological invariants. That made possible a systematic language for robust edge transport, bulk-boundary correspondence, and quantized response functions.

The TKNN result became one of the foundations of topological phases of matter. It directly shaped later work on Chern insulators, the anomalous quantum Hall effect without Landau levels, topological insulators, and topological superconductors. Its influence lies not just in explaining one phenomenon, but in establishing that topology can determine observable transport coefficients exactly, turning “topological order” and “topological band theory” into central organizing ideas in condensed-matter physics.

## Abstract

The Hall conductance of a two-dimensional electron gas has been studied in a uniform magnetic field and a periodic substrate potential $U$. The Kubo formula is written in a form that makes apparent the quantization when the Fermi energy lies in a gap. Explicit expressions have been obtained for the Hall conductance for both large and small $\frac{U}{\ensuremath{\hbar}{\ensuremath{\omega}}_{c}}$.
