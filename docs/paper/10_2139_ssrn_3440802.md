# Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System

## Why this mattered

Published under the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto in 2008, *Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System* mattered because it showed how digital money could operate without a bank, clearinghouse, or central mint. Its key shift was not any single primitive, but the combination of peer-to-peer networking, digital signatures, hash-based proof-of-work, and an incentive-compatible public ledger to make double-spending economically difficult without trusted intermediaries.

After this paper, it became possible to build a scarce, transferable digital asset whose ownership history could be verified by anyone and updated by an open network. That made “trust” a protocol property rather than an institutional relationship, extending earlier work on cryptographic cash, Hashcash, and distributed systems into a working design for decentralized consensus under adversarial conditions.

The paper’s later importance lies in the systems it enabled: Bitcoin itself, proof-of-work blockchain networks, cryptocurrency markets, programmable blockchains, decentralized finance, and a large research literature on consensus, cryptoeconomics, custody, privacy, scalability, and energy use. Craig S. Wright has claimed authorship, but the paper’s historical authorship is conventionally attributed to Satoshi Nakamoto, and a UK High Court judgment in 2024 found that Wright was not Nakamoto.
