# The automated computation of tree-level and next-to-leading order differential cross sections, and their matching to parton shower simulations

## Why this mattered

This paper mattered because it helped turn next-to-leading-order collider phenomenology from a specialist, process-by-process craft into a broadly automated workflow. Earlier tools had automated important pieces of event generation, but **MadGraph5_aMC@NLO** unified tree-level calculations, one-loop/NLO QCD corrections, subtraction, matching to parton showers, and multi-jet merging within a common user-facing framework. That changed what was practical: LHC analyses could increasingly use more accurate differential predictions for many processes without requiring each calculation to be hand-built by experts in loop amplitudes, infrared subtraction, and shower matching.

The paradigm shift was not only computational but methodological. The paper made NLO-accurate event generation resemble a standard infrastructure layer for collider physics: users specified the physics process and model inputs, while the program handled matrix-element generation, phase-space integration, event production, parallel execution, and matching to shower simulations. This lowered the barrier between formal perturbative QCD developments and experimental use, making precision simulations more routine for Standard Model measurements and new-physics searches. In that sense, it helped redefine “theory prediction” at the LHC as something that could be produced reproducibly, differentially, and with realistic event structure rather than as isolated parton-level numbers.

Its influence is visible in the subsequent normalization of automated precision tools across high-energy physics. Later developments in NLO merging, electroweak corrections, beyond-the-Standard-Model automation, and increasingly complex LHC reinterpretations built on the expectation that accurate matrix elements and showered event samples should be available in flexible public frameworks. The paper did not invent every component it assembled, but its importance lies in integrating them into a practical system at the moment when LHC physics needed high-volume, high-precision, process-flexible simulation.

## Abstract

We discuss the theoretical bases that underpin the automation of the computations of tree-level and next-to-leading order cross sections, of their matching to parton shower simulations, and of the merging of matched samples that differ by light-parton multiplicities. We present a computer program, MadGraph5 aMC@NLO, capable of handling all these computations — parton-level fixed order, shower-matched, merged — in a unified framework whose defining features are flexibility, high level of parallelisation, and human intervention limited to input physics quantities. We demonstrate the potential of the program by presenting selected phenomenological applications relevant to the LHC and to a 1-TeV e + e − collider. While next-to-leading order results are restricted to QCD corrections to SM processes in the first public version, we show that from the user viewpoint no changes have to be expected in the case of corrections due to any given renormalisable Lagrangian, and that the implementation of these are well under way.
