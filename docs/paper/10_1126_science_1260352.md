# Plastic waste inputs from land into the ocean

## Why this mattered

Before Jambeck et al. (2015), marine plastic pollution was widely visible and locally documented, but it lacked a defensible global input estimate from land-based waste. The paper shifted the field from describing debris found in oceans, beaches, and organisms to quantifying the upstream waste-management failure that supplied it. By linking coastal population, national waste generation, plastic fraction, and mismanaged waste, it made plastic leakage into the ocean a measurable systems problem rather than only an environmental observation problem.

The central result, 4.8 to 12.7 million metric tons entering the ocean in 2010 from land-based sources, gave researchers, governments, and international bodies a common scale for the crisis. Just as important, the study identified waste management quality and coastal population as the main drivers of national contributions. That reframed intervention: reducing ocean plastics was not only a matter of cleanup or consumer behavior, but of collection, disposal, recycling, and infrastructure in rapidly urbanizing coastal regions.

After this paper, later work could build scenarios, policy targets, and material-flow models around a baseline estimate of annual leakage. It helped enable subsequent global assessments of plastic production, fate, and pollution pathways, including studies that traced plastic through rivers, evaluated mitigation strategies, and projected the effects of improved waste management. Its paradigm shift was to turn marine plastic pollution into a quantified, comparable, and forecastable global mass-balance problem.

## Abstract

Plastic debris in the marine environment is widely documented, but the quantity of plastic entering the ocean from waste generated on land is unknown. By linking worldwide data on solid waste, population density, and economic status, we estimated the mass of land-based plastic waste entering the ocean. We calculate that 275 million metric tons (MT) of plastic waste was generated in 192 coastal countries in 2010, with 4.8 to 12.7 million MT entering the ocean. Population size and the quality of waste management systems largely determine which countries contribute the greatest mass of uncaptured waste available to become plastic marine debris. Without waste management infrastructure improvements, the cumulative quantity of plastic waste available to enter the ocean from land is predicted to increase by an order of magnitude by 2025.
