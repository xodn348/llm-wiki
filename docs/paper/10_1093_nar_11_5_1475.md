# Accurate transcription initiation by RNA polymerase II in a soluble extract from isolated mammalian nuclei

## Why this mattered

Before this paper, mammalian RNA polymerase II transcription was difficult to reduce to a controllable biochemical system without losing promoter specificity. Dignam, Lebovitz, and Roeder showed that soluble extracts from isolated human cell nuclei could initiate transcription accurately from Pol II promoters, especially the adenovirus 2 major late promoter, and also from other viral and cellular promoters. The shift was methodological but profound: promoter function could now be studied outside the cell while preserving the core machinery needed for correct start-site selection.

That made eukaryotic transcription experimentally dissectible. Researchers could add, remove, fractionate, or purify extract components; mutate promoter DNA; compare templates; and ask which factors were necessary for initiation. This helped turn “transcription regulation” from a mostly genetic and cellular phenomenon into a biochemical assembly problem, paving the way for identification and characterization of general transcription factors such as TFIID/TBP, TFIIA, TFIIB, TFIIE, TFIIF, and TFIIH, as well as later work on activators, coactivators, chromatin effects, and promoter architecture.

The paper’s importance also lies in its durability. The “Dignam extract” became a standard platform for in vitro transcription and related nuclear biochemistry, giving many later discoveries a reproducible assay system. It connected Roeder’s earlier work distinguishing eukaryotic RNA polymerases with the next generation of mechanistic transcription biology: not merely which polymerase copied which genes, but how a mammalian promoter recruits a multi-factor initiation apparatus and how regulatory proteins alter that process.

## Abstract

We have developed a procedure for preparing extracts from nuclei of human tissue culture cells that directs accurate transcription initiation in vitro from class II promoters. Conditions of extraction and assay have been optimized for maximum activity using the major late promoter of adenovirus 2. The extract also directs accurate transcription initiation from other adenovirus promoters and cellular promoters. The extract also directs accurate transcription initiation from class III promoters (tRNA and Ad 2 VA).
