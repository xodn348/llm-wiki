# Axicabtagene Ciloleucel CAR T-Cell Therapy in Refractory Large B-Cell Lymphoma

## Why this mattered

Before this study, refractory large B-cell lymphoma after standard chemotherapy and transplant-ineligible or post-transplant failure had a poor outlook, and most systemic therapies offered limited durable benefit. ZUMA-1 made a different therapeutic premise credible at scale: a patient’s own T cells could be engineered ex vivo to recognize CD19 and then produce deep, lasting remissions in an otherwise treatment-resistant aggressive lymphoma. The reported 82% objective response rate, 54% complete response rate, and persistence of complete responses in 40% of treated patients at a median 15.4 months showed that CAR T-cell therapy was not merely a laboratory or single-center proof of concept, but a reproducible multicenter clinical platform.

The paper mattered because it helped move cancer treatment from selecting among externally administered drugs toward manufacturing a living, patient-specific immune product. Its 99% manufacturing success rate among enrolled patients was especially important: for a personalized cellular therapy, feasibility was part of the scientific claim. At the same time, the trial defined the new clinical tradeoff. Durable remission was newly possible for some patients with refractory disease, but it came with distinctive toxicities, especially cytokine release syndrome, neurologic events, and severe cytopenias. That combination shaped the infrastructure of modern cellular therapy: specialized centers, toxicity grading and management protocols, inpatient monitoring, and later efforts to improve CAR design and supportive care.

ZUMA-1 also became part of the evidentiary bridge from experimental immunotherapy to a new oncology modality. Alongside other CD19 CAR T-cell studies, it supported regulatory approval of axi-cel and helped establish CAR T cells as a standard option in relapsed or refractory aggressive B-cell malignancies. Subsequent breakthroughs built on the same paradigm: earlier-line use in large B-cell lymphoma, CAR T products with different costimulatory domains, bispecific antibodies as off-the-shelf immune redirectors, and ongoing attempts to extend engineered cell therapy beyond CD19-positive blood cancers into other hematologic diseases and solid tumors.

## Abstract

BACKGROUND: In a phase 1 trial, axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel), an autologous anti-CD19 chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy, showed efficacy in patients with refractory large B-cell lymphoma after the failure of conventional therapy. METHODS: anti-CD19 CAR T cells per kilogram of body weight after receiving a conditioning regimen of low-dose cyclophosphamide and fludarabine. The primary end point was the rate of objective response (calculated as the combined rates of complete response and partial response). Secondary end points included overall survival, safety, and biomarker assessments. RESULTS: Among the 111 patients who were enrolled, axi-cel was successfully manufactured for 110 (99%) and administered to 101 (91%). The objective response rate was 82%, and the complete response rate was 54%.With a median follow-up of 15.4 months, 42% of the patients continued to have a response, with 40% continuing to have a complete response. The overall rate of survival at 18 months was 52%. The most common adverse events of grade 3 or higher during treatment were neutropenia (in 78% of the patients), anemia (in 43%), and thrombocytopenia (in 38%). Grade 3 or higher cytokine release syndrome and neurologic events occurred in 13% and 28% of the patients, respectively. Three of the patients died during treatment. Higher CAR T-cell levels in blood were associated with response. CONCLUSIONS: In this multicenter study, patients with refractory large B-cell lymphoma who received CAR T-cell therapy with axi-cel had high levels of durable response, with a safety profile that included myelosuppression, the cytokine release syndrome, and neurologic events. (Funded by Kite Pharma and the Leukemia and Lymphoma Society Therapy Acceleration Program; ZUMA-1 ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT02348216 .).
