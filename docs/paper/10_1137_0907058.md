# GMRES: A Generalized Minimal Residual Algorithm for Solving Nonsymmetric Linear Systems

## Why this mattered

GMRES mattered because it made Krylov-subspace iteration broadly practical for nonsymmetric linear systems, a class that arises naturally in discretized fluid dynamics, transport, electromagnetics, circuit simulation, and many other scientific-computing problems. Before this work, the conjugate-gradient paradigm had shown the power of short-recurrence Krylov methods for symmetric positive definite systems, and MINRES extended residual minimization to symmetric indefinite systems. Saad and Schultz’s contribution was to give a clear Arnoldi-based algorithm that minimized the residual norm at every step over the generated Krylov subspace for general nonsymmetric matrices. That made “best available residual reduction in the current Krylov space” a usable computational principle rather than mainly a formal equivalence to methods such as GCR or ORTHODIR.

The paper also shifted attention from exact direct factorization toward scalable iterative solvers whose effectiveness could be combined with preconditioning. GMRES did not remove the hard problem of nonsymmetric linear algebra: storage and orthogonalization costs grow with the iteration count, and convergence can depend strongly on spectral and nonnormal behavior. But by making restarted variants and preconditioned implementations natural, it gave numerical analysts and application scientists a robust default framework for large sparse nonsymmetric systems. After GMRES, progress in sparse linear solvers increasingly centered on designing preconditioners, restart strategies, flexible variants, and domain-specific Krylov methods rather than treating nonsymmetry as a reason to fall back on direct methods.

Its influence can be seen in later breakthroughs such as restarted GMRES, flexible GMRES for changing preconditioners, deflated and augmented Krylov methods, and modern solver packages used in large-scale PDE simulation. The paper helped establish the Arnoldi process as a central organizing tool for nonsymmetric matrix computations, not only for linear solves but also for eigenvalue algorithms and model reduction. In that sense, GMRES became more than a single algorithm: it became a template for how to turn projection onto Krylov subspaces into reliable numerical machinery for problems too large or too irregular for classical direct approaches.

## Abstract

We present an iterative method for solving linear systems, which has the property ofminimizing at every step the norm of the residual vector over a Krylov subspace. The algorithm is derived from the Arnoldi process for constructing an l2-orthogonal basis of Krylov subspaces. It can be considered as a generalization of Paige and Saunders’ MINRES algorithm and is theoretically equivalent to the Generalized Conjugate Residual (GCR) method and to ORTHODIR. The new algorithm presents several advantages over GCR and ORTHODIR.
