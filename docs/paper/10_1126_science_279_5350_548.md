# Triblock Copolymer Syntheses of Mesoporous Silica with Periodic 50 to 300 Angstrom Pores

## Why this mattered

Before this paper, ordered mesoporous silicas were already a major advance, but their pore sizes and hydrothermal stability limited what they could host or withstand. Zhao and coauthors showed that nonionic amphiphilic triblock copolymers could template silica into highly ordered SBA-15 with unusually large, tunable, uniform mesopores and thick silica walls. The key shift was not only “bigger pores,” but a broader synthesis principle: block-copolymer self-assembly could organize inorganic frameworks over length scales far beyond those accessible with small surfactants while preserving periodic order.

That made mesoporous silica a more practical platform for chemistry involving larger species: biomolecules, polymers, nanoparticles, bulky catalysts, and controlled-release cargo. The thick walls and reported stability in boiling water also changed expectations for durability, helping move ordered mesoporous materials from structural curiosities toward usable supports, hosts, membranes, adsorbents, and catalyst architectures. SBA-15 became a reference material because its pore diameter, wall thickness, and surface chemistry could be adjusted independently enough to support systematic design.

The paper also helped connect soft-matter self-assembly with inorganic materials synthesis in a way that shaped later work on templated oxides, hierarchical porous solids, nanocasting, drug-delivery carriers, and supported nanocatalysts. Subsequent breakthroughs often relied on the possibility this work made routine: building ordered inorganic frameworks around removable polymer templates to create stable, accessible nanoscale space on demand.

## Abstract

Use of amphiphilic triblock copolymers to direct the organization of polymerizing silica species has resulted in the preparation of well-ordered hexagonal mesoporous silica structures (SBA-15) with uniform pore sizes up to approximately 300 angstroms. The SBA-15 materials are synthesized in acidic media to produce highly ordered, two-dimensional hexagonal (space group p6mm) silica-block copolymer mesophases. Calcination at 500 degrees C gives porous structures with unusually large interlattice d spacings of 74.5 to 320 angstroms between the (100) planes, pore sizes from 46 to 300 angstroms, pore volume fractions up to 0.85, and silica wall thicknesses of 31 to 64 angstroms. SBA-15 can be readily prepared over a wide range of uniform pore sizes and pore wall thicknesses at low temperature (35 degrees to 80 degrees C), using a variety of poly(alkylene oxide) triblock copolymers and by the addition of cosolvent organic molecules. The block copolymer species can be recovered for reuse by solvent extraction with ethanol or removed by heating at 140 degrees C for 3 hours, in both cases, yielding a product that is thermally stable in boiling water.
