# Natural RNA circles function as efficient microRNA sponges

## Why this mattered

Before this work, circular RNAs in animals were mostly regarded as rare splice oddities or poorly understood byproducts. Hansen and colleagues showed that a naturally expressed circular RNA, ciRS-7/CDR1as, had a specific regulatory role: it carried dozens of conserved miR-7 binding sites, associated with Argonaute in a miR-7-dependent way, and could inhibit miR-7 activity without being rapidly destroyed like ordinary linear targets ([Nature, 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11993)). That shifted circRNAs from biochemical curiosities into candidate functional regulators of gene expression.

The paper mattered because it made a new kind of post-transcriptional control experimentally concrete. A single stable RNA circle could act as a high-capacity sink for a microRNA, linking circRNA biology to the broader competing-endogenous-RNA and target-mimicry ideas already emerging for linear transcripts. Its impact was amplified by the contemporaneous Rajewsky-lab paper showing that circRNAs were a large animal RNA class and that CDR1as was densely bound by miRNA effector complexes ([Nature, 2013](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11928)).

What became newly possible was a systematic search for circRNA function: not just cataloguing back-spliced transcripts, but asking whether particular circles regulate miRNAs, bind proteins, mark cell states, or contribute to disease. Later work complicated the original “circRNAs as sponges” model, showing that ciRS-7 was exceptional rather than representative of all circRNAs, but that qualification is part of the paper’s legacy: it launched a field precise enough to distinguish rare high-capacity sponges from the many circRNAs with other or still-unknown roles.
