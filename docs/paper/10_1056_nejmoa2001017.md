# 다양한 다공질 Calcium Polyphosphate의 생분해성에 관한 장기적인 연구

## Why this mattered

The supplied metadata and abstract describe different papers: the title refers to a 2003 Korean periodontal-materials study on biodegradable porous calcium polyphosphate, while the abstract is from the early 2020 report identifying 2019-nCoV in Wuhan pneumonia patients. Taken literally, the provided record cannot support a “Fleming-tier” claim for the 2003 calcium-polyphosphate paper, especially with 0 citations and no abstract about that material study.

If the intended paper is the coronavirus report described in the abstract, its importance lay in showing that unbiased sequencing, paired with viral isolation in human airway epithelial cells, could rapidly convert an unexplained pneumonia cluster into a defined infectious-disease event. The paper made it possible to name the pathogen, place it phylogenetically within sarbecoviruses, and distinguish it from SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV. That shifted the response from syndromic surveillance of “unknown pneumonia” toward targeted diagnostics, genomic epidemiology, and coronavirus-specific public-health investigation.

This identification became the foundation for subsequent breakthroughs: sequence-based PCR testing, global genome sharing, variant tracking, and vaccine design all depended on knowing the causative virus and its relationship to other coronaviruses. Its paradigm shift was not a new biological theory, but a demonstration of a modern outbreak-discovery pipeline: clinical cluster recognition, unbiased sequencing, cell-culture isolation, phylogenetic classification, and immediate surveillance escalation.

## Abstract

In December 2019, a cluster of patients with pneumonia of unknown cause was linked to a seafood wholesale market in Wuhan, China. A previously unknown betacoronavirus was discovered through the use of unbiased sequencing in samples from patients with pneumonia. Human airway epithelial cells were used to isolate a novel coronavirus, named 2019-nCoV, which formed a clade within the subgenus sarbecovirus, Orthocoronavirinae subfamily. Different from both MERS-CoV and SARS-CoV, 2019-nCoV is the seventh member of the family of coronaviruses that infect humans. Enhanced surveillance and further investigation are ongoing. (Funded by the National Key Research and Development Program of China and the National Major Project for Control and Prevention of Infectious Disease in China.).
