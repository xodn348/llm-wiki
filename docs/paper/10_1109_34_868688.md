# Normalized cuts and image segmentation

## Why this mattered

Shi and Malik’s paper mattered because it reframed image segmentation from a primarily local boundary-finding or region-growing problem into a global graph partitioning problem. In their formulation, pixels or image regions became nodes, affinities became weighted edges, and a “good” segmentation was one that separated weakly connected groups while avoiding the trivial solution of cutting off small isolated sets. The normalized cut criterion was the key shift: it measured a partition relative to the total association of each side with the whole graph, making segmentation a problem of balanced perceptual grouping rather than merely minimizing boundary cost.

The paper also made this global view computationally usable. By connecting the normalized cut objective to a generalized eigenvalue problem, Shi and Malik gave vision researchers a practical spectral method for approximating perceptually meaningful partitions in static images and motion sequences. This helped establish spectral graph methods as a central tool in computer vision, influencing later work in image segmentation, clustering, semi-supervised learning, graph-based data analysis, and manifold methods.

Its longer-term importance is that it treated visual organization as an affinity structure over data, not just as a set of local measurements. That perspective became foundational for later segmentation systems that reason over regions, superpixels, object proposals, and learned similarity graphs. Although modern deep networks now learn many of the affinities and representations that earlier systems hand-designed, the normalized-cuts paper helped define the problem language: segmentation as grouping under a global objective, with graph structure as the bridge between low-level measurements and coherent visual objects.

## Abstract

We propose a novel approach for solving the perceptual grouping problem in vision. Rather than focusing on local features and their consistencies in the image data, our approach aims at extracting the global impression of an image. We treat image segmentation as a graph partitioning problem and propose a novel global criterion, the normalized cut, for segmenting the graph. The normalized cut criterion measures both the total dissimilarity between the different groups as well as the total similarity within the groups. We show that an efficient computational technique based on a generalized eigenvalue problem can be used to optimize this criterion. We applied this approach to segmenting static images, as well as motion sequences, and found the results to be very encouraging.
