# Orbital angular momentum of light and the transformation of Laguerre-Gaussian laser modes

## Why this mattered

Allen, Beijersbergen, Spreeuw, and Woerdman’s 1992 paper made a decisive conceptual separation between the spin angular momentum of light, associated with polarization, and orbital angular momentum, associated with the spatial structure of the optical field. By showing that Laguerre-Gaussian modes with azimuthal phase factor carry a well-defined orbital angular momentum per photon, the paper turned what had been a mathematical feature of laser modes into a physically measurable property of light. Its proposed torque experiment also framed this angular momentum as mechanically transferable, not merely formal.

The paradigm shift was that light’s transverse mode structure became an active degree of freedom for physics and engineering. After this paper, optical beams could be designed not only by intensity, phase, wavelength, and polarization, but also by quantized spatial angular momentum. The reversible transformation between Laguerre-Gaussian and Hermite-Gaussian modes showed that this property could be generated, converted, and analyzed with ordinary optical systems, helping make orbital angular momentum experimentally accessible rather than a theoretical curiosity.

This opened paths that later became central in optical manipulation, quantum optics, and communications. Optical tweezers and micromanipulation gained a route to exert controlled torques on microscopic objects; quantum experiments gained a high-dimensional encoding variable beyond polarization; and classical optical communications explored spatial modes as multiplexing channels. The paper mattered because it established orbital angular momentum as a usable, conserved, and engineerable property of light, launching a field around structured light and optical vortices.

## Abstract

Laser light with a Laguerre-Gaussian amplitude distribution is found to have a well-defined orbital angular momentum. An astigmatic optical system may be used to transform a high-order Laguerre-Gaussian mode into a high-order Hermite-Gaussian mode reversibly. An experiment is proposed to measure the mechanical torque induced by the transfer of orbital angular momentum associated with such a transformation.
