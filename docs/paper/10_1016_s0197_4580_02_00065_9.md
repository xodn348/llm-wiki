# Staging of brain pathology related to sporadic Parkinson’s disease

## Why this mattered

Braak et al. mattered because it reframed sporadic Parkinson’s disease as a staged, anatomically progressive disorder rather than a condition defined chiefly by late degeneration of the substantia nigra. By mapping Lewy-related pathology across postmortem brains, the paper proposed that pathology often begins in vulnerable lower brainstem and olfactory structures, then ascends through brainstem nuclei, midbrain, limbic regions, and eventually neocortex. This made non-motor symptoms such as hyposmia, constipation, sleep disturbance, and autonomic dysfunction intelligible as early disease features rather than peripheral curiosities.

The staging scheme gave Parkinson’s research a spatial and temporal model that could be tested against clinical progression, imaging, neuropathology, and biomarker studies. It helped make possible the later focus on prodromal Parkinson’s disease: identifying people before classical motor diagnosis, tracking risk states such as REM sleep behavior disorder, and searching for biomarkers of alpha-synuclein pathology outside the substantia nigra. Even where later work complicated the model, including cases that do not follow a simple caudo-rostral sequence, the paper changed the question from “where is Parkinson’s damage most visible?” to “where and when does the disease process begin?”

Its influence also shaped later breakthroughs in alpha-synuclein propagation, gut-brain and olfactory hypotheses, and attempts to detect misfolded alpha-synuclein in peripheral tissues or biofluids. The Braak staging proposal did not prove a single route of causation, but it supplied a coherent clinicopathological scaffold: Parkinson’s could be studied as a long, distributed process with detectable prediagnostic phases. That shift remains central to disease-modifying strategies, because therapies aimed at slowing or preventing Parkinson’s require a framework for finding the disease before irreversible motor circuitry loss has already occurred.
