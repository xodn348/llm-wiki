# A Heteroskedasticity-Consistent Covariance Matrix Estimator and a Direct Test for Heteroskedasticity

## Why this mattered

White’s 1980 paper changed the default meaning of inference in linear regression. Before it, applied researchers often had to either assume homoskedastic errors or specify a particular structure for heteroskedasticity before standard errors could be trusted. White showed that valid large-sample covariance estimates could be obtained without modeling the form of the error variance. That made regression inference more robust: coefficients estimated by ordinary least squares could still be accompanied by asymptotically valid standard errors even when the classical constant-variance assumption failed.

The paradigm shift was methodological as much as technical. Heteroskedasticity stopped being only a diagnostic problem requiring correction through a chosen variance model, and became something inference procedures could be designed to tolerate. The paper also supplied a direct test for heteroskedasticity by comparing the usual covariance estimator with the new heteroskedasticity-consistent one, linking robustness and diagnosis in a single framework. This helped move econometrics toward “model-robust” practice, where researchers could report credible uncertainty estimates under weaker assumptions.

Its influence is visible in nearly every later development in empirical economics and applied statistics that treats standard errors as a central object of design: robust standard errors, sandwich estimators, cluster-robust covariance matrices, generalized method of moments inference, and modern semiparametric and causal-estimation workflows all build on the same broad idea. White’s estimator did not eliminate the need for good research design or correct specification of conditional means, but it made a new kind of empirical work possible: routine regression-based inference in messy observational settings without pretending that error variances were known, constant, or easily parameterized.

## Abstract

This paper presents a parameter covariance matrix estimator which is consistent even when the disturbances of a linear regression model are heteroskedastic. This estimator does not depend on a formal model of the structure of the heteroskedasticity. By comparing the elements of the new estimator to those of the usual covariance estimator, one obtains a direct test for heteroskedasticity, since in the absence of heteroskedasticity, the two estimators will be approximately equal, but will generally diverge otherwise. The test has an appealing least squares interpretation
