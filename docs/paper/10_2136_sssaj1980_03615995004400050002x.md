# A Closed‐form Equation for Predicting the Hydraulic Conductivity of Unsaturated Soils

## Why this mattered

van Genuchten’s 1980 paper mattered because it turned an experimentally difficult soil-physics problem into a usable predictive framework. Before this work, unsaturated hydraulic conductivity was hard to measure directly and awkward to represent mathematically, especially across the wide range of water contents relevant to infiltration, drainage, evaporation, and plant water uptake. The paper introduced a smooth soil-water retention equation whose particular form could be inserted into the Burdine and Mualem pore-size distribution models to yield closed-form expressions for relative hydraulic conductivity. That combination made it possible to estimate conductivity functions from retention-curve parameters rather than requiring extensive conductivity measurements.

The paradigm shift was not merely a better curve fit; it was the coupling of water retention and hydraulic conductivity in a compact parameterization that could be used analytically and computationally. After this paper, unsaturated flow models could represent different soils with a small set of fitted parameters, enabling more practical simulation of variably saturated flow under the Richards equation. This became foundational for hydrology, soil physics, vadose-zone contaminant transport, irrigation science, and land-surface modeling, where the van Genuchten–Mualem formulation is still a standard way to encode soil hydraulic behavior.

Its influence also lies in how it made later breakthroughs scalable. Numerical models such as HYDRUS and related vadose-zone simulators could use van Genuchten parameters as portable, interpretable inputs; pedotransfer functions could estimate those parameters from soil texture and bulk properties; and larger Earth-system and watershed models could incorporate unsaturated hydraulic behavior without measuring full conductivity curves for every soil. The paper’s own caution remains important: conductivity predictions depend strongly on how well the low-water-content part of the retention curve is described, a limitation that shaped later work on parameter estimation, hysteresis, dual-porosity flow, and improved soil hydraulic databases.

## Abstract

Abstract A new and relatively simple equation for the soil‐water content‐pressure head curve, θ( h ), is described in this paper. The particular form of the equation enables one to derive closed‐form analytical expressions for the relative hydraulic conductivity, K r , when substituted in the predictive conductivity models of N.T. Burdine or Y. Mualem. The resulting expressions for K r ( h ) contain three independent parameters which may be obtained by fitting the proposed soil‐water retention model to experimental data. Results obtained with the closed‐form analytical expressions based on the Mualem theory are compared with observed hydraulic conductivity data for five soils with a wide range of hydraulic properties. The unsaturated hydraulic conductivity is predicted well in four out of five cases. It is found that a reasonable description of the soil‐water retention curve at low water contents is important for an accurated prediction of the unsaturated hydraulic conductivity.
