# The Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) pilot analysis: Multitissue gene regulation in humans

## Why this mattered

Before GTEx, human regulatory genetics was often constrained to accessible cell types or a small number of tissues, leaving a basic uncertainty: whether a disease-associated variant affected gene expression broadly, only in the relevant organ, or not detectably in the sampled tissue at all. The GTEx pilot shifted that problem from anecdote to atlas-building. By pairing genotype data with RNA-seq across many postmortem tissues from the same donors, it showed that human expression regulation could be mapped systematically across the body, distinguishing shared eQTLs from tissue-specific effects and linking some GWAS signals to plausible regulatory mechanisms.

The paper mattered because it made “which tissue?” a central question in interpreting noncoding genetic variation. Most common disease-associated variants discovered by GWAS lay outside protein-coding sequence; GTEx provided a practical route from variant to gene, tissue, and regulatory hypothesis. It did not solve causality by itself, and the pilot was limited by sample size and tissue composition, but it established the reference framework that later, larger GTEx releases, colocalization methods, transcriptome-wide association studies, and disease fine-mapping pipelines would build on.

Its broader paradigm shift was to treat gene regulation as a human, organism-wide quantitative trait rather than as a property inferred from a few cell lines. After this work, a genetic association could be evaluated against a public multitissue regulatory map, making it newly possible to prioritize candidate causal genes, identify disease-relevant tissues, and ask whether regulatory architecture was shared or context-specific. That changed the default interpretation of human genetic variation: variants were no longer just positions in a genome-wide scan, but potential perturbations of molecular programs whose effects depended on tissue context.

## Abstract

Expression, genetic variation, and tissues Human genomes show extensive genetic variation across individuals, but we have only just started documenting the effects of this variation on the regulation of gene expression. Furthermore, only a few tissues have been examined per genetic variant. In order to examine how genetic expression varies among tissues within individuals, the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) Consortium collected 1641 postmortem samples covering 54 body sites from 175 individuals. They identified quantitative genetic traits that affect gene expression and determined which of these exhibit tissue-specific expression patterns. Melé et al. measured how transcription varies among tissues, and Rivas et al. looked at how truncated protein variants affect expression across tissues.
