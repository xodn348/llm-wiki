# The Interpretation of Cultures

## Why this mattered

Strictly speaking, the 1974 *Journal for the Scientific Study of Religion* item is a review record for Clifford Geertz’s 1973 book *The Interpretation of Cultures*, not a co-authored research paper by Richard K. Fenn and Geertz. Its importance lies in the book’s consolidation of interpretive anthropology: culture was not treated chiefly as a set of causal variables, social functions, or evolutionary survivals, but as a public system of symbols through which people make meaning. Geertz’s program made “thick description” a methodological ideal, shifting explanation toward close interpretation of situated practices.

After this work, scholars had a clearer license to study rituals, religious symbols, politics, kinship, and everyday action as texts to be interpreted without reducing them to psychology, economics, or social structure alone. For the study of religion, this was especially consequential: religion could be analyzed as a cultural system that shapes moods, motivations, worldviews, and social order through symbols, rather than only as belief, institution, or social function. The approach helped open later work in symbolic anthropology, cultural sociology, religious studies, cultural history, and qualitative social science, while also provoking important critiques about power, colonial authority, and the limits of interpretation that shaped subsequent post-structural, reflexive, and practice-oriented scholarship.
