# Effects of Configuration Interaction on Intensities and Phase Shifts

## Why this mattered

Fano’s 1961 paper turned asymmetric spectral lines from an anomaly into a general diagnostic of quantum interference. Before it, resonances were often treated as isolated Lorentzian peaks associated with relatively well-defined states. Fano showed that when a discrete autoionizing state and a continuum lead to the same final channel, the observed intensity is governed by their interference, producing the now-standard asymmetric profile controlled by the resonance energy, width, and asymmetry parameter. This made the *shape* of a spectral feature physically interpretable, not just its position or area.

The paper mattered because it provided a compact theory for extracting microscopic information from spectra in systems where bound-state and continuum descriptions are inseparable. Its treatment of helium’s `2s2p ¹P` autoionizing resonance demonstrated that configuration interaction could be read quantitatively from experimental line shapes, including resonance widths and oscillator strengths. By extending the framework to multiple continua and multiple discrete states, Fano also made the method portable beyond one atomic example, linking atomic spectroscopy to the broader resonance-scattering language already important in nuclear physics.

The later impact was unusually broad: “Fano resonances” became a recurring signature of coherent coupling between localized and extended states. The same interference logic appears in molecular photodissociation, electron and photon scattering, semiconductor quantum wells, mesoscopic transport, plasmonic nanostructures, metamaterials, and photonic crystals. In that sense, the paradigm shift was not only a new formula for an asymmetric peak, but a new way to recognize and engineer continuum-mediated quantum interference across physical systems.

## Abstract

The interference of a discrete autoionized state with a continuum gives rise to characteristically asymmetric peaks in excitation spectra. The earlier qualitative interpretation of this phenomenon is extended and revised. A theoretical formula is fitted to the shape of the $2s2p^{1}P$ resonance of He observed in the inelastic scattering of electrons. The fitting determines the parameters of the $2s2p^{1}P$ resonance as follows: $E=60.1$ ev, $\ensuremath{\Gamma}\ensuremath{\sim}0.04$ ev, $f\ensuremath{\sim}2 \mathrm{to} 4\ifmmode\times\else\texttimes\fi{}{10}^{\ensuremath{-}3}$. The theory is extended to the interaction of one discrete level with two or more continua and of a set of discrete levels with one continuum. The theory can also give the position and intensity shifts produced in a Rydberg series of discrete levels by interaction with a level of another configuration. The connection with the nuclear theory of resonance scattering is indicated.
