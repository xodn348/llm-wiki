# A Stepwise Huisgen Cycloaddition Process: Copper(I)-Catalyzed Regioselective “Ligation” of Azides and Terminal Alkynes

## Why this mattered

Before this work, the Huisgen azide–alkyne cycloaddition was known but not broadly useful as a general ligation method: it usually required heat, gave mixtures of 1,4- and 1,5-disubstituted triazoles, and was poorly matched to complex, water-rich, functional-group-dense settings. Rostovtsev, Green, Fokin, and Sharpless changed that by showing that copper(I) catalysis makes terminal alkynes and organic azides react rapidly, cleanly, and selectively in water to give the 1,4-disubstituted 1,2,3-triazole. The shift was not merely a better yield or milder condition; it turned a classical cycloaddition into a modular bond-forming operation whose reliability, selectivity, and tolerance made it behave like a chemical connector.

That reliability helped define what “click chemistry” meant in practice. After this paper, azides and terminal alkynes became mutually orthogonal handles that chemists could install on small molecules, polymers, surfaces, biomolecules, and materials, then join under simple conditions without redesigning the rest of the system. The triazole product was stable, polar, and often biologically tolerated, so the reaction became useful not only for synthesis but for tagging, immobilization, library construction, polymer functionalization, and chemical biology. It made covalent assembly programmable in a way that previous broadly used organic reactions generally were not.

The paper also set up later breakthroughs by making the strengths and limitations of copper-catalyzed azide–alkyne cycloaddition clear. Its success drove rapid adoption across drug discovery, proteomics, glycobiology, materials science, and surface chemistry, while the toxicity and biological incompatibility of copper in some living systems motivated copper-free strain-promoted azide–alkyne cycloadditions and other bioorthogonal ligation strategies. In that sense, the 2002 reaction was both a practical tool and a conceptual benchmark: subsequent bioorthogonal chemistry was often measured against its combination of selectivity, modularity, and operational simplicity.
