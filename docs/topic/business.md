# Business

_31 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These Fleming-tier papers frame business as a problem of coordination under imperfect information, conflicting incentives, and institutional constraint. One dominant lineage runs through collective action, exit and voice, lemons markets, transaction-cost economics, agency theory, ownership-control separation, and law-and-finance: firms and markets are not frictionless allocation machines, but governance systems shaped by information asymmetry, monitoring costs, contracts, property rights, and legal institutions. A second lineage recasts the firm from a production function into a bundle of resources, knowledge, routines, managerial interpretations, and dynamic capabilities, linking competitive advantage to what organizations know, own, learn, and reconfigure over time. Finance contributes its own paradigm shifts through option pricing, capital-structure theory, asymmetric-information financing, and comparative ownership, making corporate liability, borrowing, and control analyzable as priced claims and governance arrangements.

The cluster also shows business expanding beyond the firm into networks, customers, technology, policy, and society. Weak ties and social capital connect business strategy to sociology; internationalization theory ties firm growth to learning and incremental foreign commitments; service quality, brand equity, relationship marketing, and service-dominant logic shift marketing from transactions toward perceived value, trust, and co-creation. Technology acceptance, ERP selection, and Bitcoin connect organizational adoption, enterprise systems, and decentralized monetary design to information systems and computer science. Public policy and ecosystem-services papers place business within agenda-setting, natural capital, and environmental valuation, while the mRNA vaccine paper reflects the modern fusion of business, biotechnology, regulation, platform manufacturing, and public health. Collectively, the topic represents business as an interdisciplinary science of organizations, markets, value creation, and institutional adaptation.
