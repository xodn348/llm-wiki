# Business

_31 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These Fleming-tier business papers collectively mark the shift from treating firms and markets as simple optimizing machines to analyzing them as institutions shaped by information, incentives, governance, relationships, and capabilities. One dominant lineage runs through collective action, lemons markets, exit and voice, transaction costs, agency theory, ownership-control separation, asymmetric financing, corporate borrowing, and law-and-finance: markets and firms are not frictionless, but depend on credible information, enforceable contracts, governance structures, and legal institutions. A second lineage develops strategy as an internal, evolutionary theory of advantage, moving from the resource-based view to knowledge-based theory and dynamic capabilities. A third links firms to social structure through weak ties, social capital, upper echelons, internationalization, and policy agenda-setting, showing that networks, leadership cognition, learning, and institutional contexts shape organizational outcomes.

The major paradigm shifts are therefore institutional, informational, relational, and capability-based. Finance is transformed by option pricing, capital-structure theory, ownership studies, and legal origins; marketing moves from transactions to service quality, brand equity, relationship trust, and service-dominant logic; information systems enter through technology acceptance and enterprise-system selection; and international business becomes a theory of knowledge accumulation and staged commitment. The cluster also extends business into adjacent fields: economics and political science via public goods, policy agendas, and governance; sociology via networks and social capital; law via investor protection; ecology via ecosystem services and natural capital; computer science and monetary economics via Bitcoin; and biomedical innovation via the BNT162b2 vaccine, a reminder that business institutions organize, finance, scale, and govern paradigm-shifting technologies beyond the firm itself.
