# Sociology

_30 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

The Fleming-tier sociology cluster represents a broad shift from society as a fixed structure to society as an ongoing production of meaning, classification, practice, and power. One lineage runs from Weberian organization and rationalization through institutional theory: *The Theory of Social and Economic Organization*, “The Iron Cage Revisited,” organizational learning, entrepreneurship, and cross-national culture studies trace how authority, fields, norms, and institutions shape action. A second lineage centers interpretation and everyday order: *The Social Construction of Reality*, *Studies in Ethnomethodology*, grounded theory, *The Interpretation of Cultures*, and *Frame Analysis* established social life as something made intelligible through interaction, symbols, methods, and situated accounts rather than only through external variables.

The major paradigm shifts are methodological, cultural, and political. Fisher’s work on multiple measurements sits at the statistical edge of classification, while grounded theory and case-study theory legitimated inductive qualitative research. Foucault’s *Discipline and Punish*, Habermas’s public sphere, Anderson’s nationalism, Beck’s risk society, and McLuhan’s media theory expanded sociology toward power, communication, modernity, and collective imagination. Bourdieu’s practice theory connected structure and agency; feminist, intersectional, and Black feminist works transformed identity, knowledge, and standpoint into central analytic problems. Adjacent fields are deeply entangled: economics, anthropology, media studies, education, organization studies, science and technology studies, political theory, gender studies, and cognitive science all appear as neighboring domains through which sociology became a general theory of socially produced order.
