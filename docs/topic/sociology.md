# Sociology

_30 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These Fleming-tier sociology papers collectively trace a shift from society as an ordered system of roles, institutions, and classifications to society as an ongoing accomplishment of meaning, practice, power, and situated knowledge. One lineage runs through Weberian organization and rationalization, from *The Theory of Social and Economic Organization* to institutional isomorphism, organizational learning, entrepreneurship, and cross-national cultural comparison. Another develops interpretive and micro-sociological traditions: social construction, grounded theory, ethnomethodology, cultural interpretation, frame analysis, and situated cognition all recast social order as produced through categories, interaction, local practice, and shared but contestable understandings. The 1936 work on multiple measurements, though rooted in taxonomy and statistics, signals the methodological importance of classification, measurement, and multivariate reasoning that later social science repeatedly adapts.

The major paradigm shifts are from structural functional and bureaucratic models toward constructionist, interpretive, practice-based, and critical accounts of modernity. McLuhan links social organization to media environments; Foucault relocates institutions in regimes of discipline and surveillance; Bourdieu connects culture, embodiment, and domination through practice; Habermas and Anderson reinterpret publics and nations as historically formed communicative and imagined orders; Beck reframes modern society around manufactured risk. Feminist, intersectional, and Black feminist works further transform the field by showing identity, knowledge, violence, and standpoint as structured by overlapping power relations rather than single social categories. The cluster connects sociology outward to anthropology, economics, education, organization studies, feminist theory, media studies, political theory, science and technology studies, and cognitive science, making “the social” a cross-disciplinary problem of meaning, institutions, practice, and power.
