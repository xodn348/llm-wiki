# Psychology

_62 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These Fleming-tier psychology papers collectively trace the field’s movement from laboratory measures of cognition and behavior toward a layered science of mind, self, society, and institutions. Early anchors include interference in verbal reactions, human motivation, social comparison, construct validity, and the “magical number seven,” marking the rise of experimental cognition, psychometrics, and information-processing models. Mid-century clinical and assessment papers on anxiety, depression, cognitive status, functional ability, stress, and life events helped standardize psychological states as measurable constructs. In parallel, social psychology expanded from interpersonal relations, self-presentation, stigma, locus of control, self-efficacy, belonging, culture, and planned behavior into a theory-rich account of agency, identity, motivation, and social influence.

The dominant paradigm shifts are methodological as much as substantive: construct validation, multitrait-multimethod reasoning, structural equation modeling, moderator-mediator analysis, latent-variable executive-function models, and later warnings about false positives reshaped standards of inference. The cluster also shows psychology’s porous borders. Grounded theory, conversation analysis, social networks, organizational legitimacy, job strain, psychological safety, technology acceptance, absorptive capacity, and collective efficacy connect it to sociology, management, education, criminology, and information systems. The biopsychosocial model, affect circumplex, perceived exertion, stereotaxic brain mapping, default-mode function, cortical connectivity, and phonetics software link it to medicine, neuroscience, physiology, and linguistics. By 2000 onward, positive psychology, emerging adulthood, construal-level theory, WEIRD-sample critique, Mechanical Turk, and reproducibility concerns reveal a field simultaneously broadening its subject matter and interrogating the evidential foundations of its own methods.
