# Psychology

_62 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These Fleming-tier psychology papers collectively trace the field’s movement from laboratory measurement of attention and interference toward a broad science of mind, self, society, health, and institutions. Early anchors such as the Stroop interference study, Maslow’s motivation theory, Miller’s “magical number seven,” and construct-validity work established psychology as both experimental and psychometric: concerned with observable performance, latent traits, and the validity of measurement. Mid-century social psychology then reframed persons as socially situated actors, through social comparison, impression management, interpersonal attribution, stigma, locus of control, belonging, culture, and planned behavior. In parallel, clinical and health-oriented papers created standardized instruments for anxiety, depression, daily functioning, cognitive status, stress, perceived exertion, and well-being, helping make psychological states measurable in medicine and public health.

The later lineage shows several paradigm shifts: from trait and symptom description to cognitive-processing models; from individual behavior to ecological, organizational, network, and neighborhood contexts; from biomedical reductionism to biopsychosocial explanation; and from universalist claims to cultural and methodological scrutiny. Self-efficacy, collective efficacy, psychological safety, organizational commitment, job demands, absorptive capacity, and user acceptance connect psychology to education, management, sociology, criminology, and information systems. Executive function, default-mode activity, cortical connectivity, and stereotaxic brain mapping link the topic to neuroscience, while grounded theory, conversation analysis, Mechanical Turk, Praat, and “False-Positive Psychology” mark methodological expansions and crises. The cluster therefore represents psychology not as a single paradigm, but as a set of measurement traditions and explanatory frameworks for behavior across brains, persons, groups, cultures, and institutions.
