# Economics

_37 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These Fleming-tier economics papers collectively trace the field’s movement from grand macroeconomic explanation to a plural science of institutions, incentives, uncertainty, and empirical identification. Keynes’s *General Theory* anchors the macroeconomic lineage by recasting unemployment, interest, and aggregate demand as systemic problems rather than market-clearing anomalies. Growth and development then branch through Lewis, Solow, Swan-style aggregate production logic, technical change, increasing returns, endogenous innovation, and cross-country growth empirics. Public economics and political economy enter through local expenditures and democracy, while institutional economics runs from Coase’s firm to Williamson’s capitalism, North’s institutional change, and Esping-Andersen’s welfare regimes.

A second dominant lineage is the reconstruction of markets under information, risk, and bounded rationality. Asset-pricing papers develop equilibrium risk-return models, empirical tests, corporate debt pricing, cross-sectional factors, common risk factors, and momentum anomalies, linking economics to finance and statistics. Spence, Jensen and Meckling, Stiglitz-Weiss, hedonic pricing, Becker’s crime model, and Granovetter’s embeddedness show how contracts, signals, agencies, social structure, and implicit markets reshape standard choice theory. Econometrics becomes a paradigm in its own right through nonparametric trend tests, Granger causality, specification testing, VAR macroeconomics, ARCH volatility, and cointegration. Prospect theory and fairness models connect economics to psychology and behavioral science, while evolutionary economics links it to biology-like models of adaptation. The 2016 policy-uncertainty paper marks a later synthesis: macro, finance, politics, and measurement converging around quantifiable institutional risk.
