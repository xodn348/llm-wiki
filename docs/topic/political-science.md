# Political science

_10 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These papers collectively trace a central political-science arc from institutions and participation to identity, information, and planetary governance. “The Tragedy of the Commons” framed collective action failure as a core problem of modern politics, while “Governing the Commons” shifted the paradigm by showing that communities can sometimes build durable institutions beyond simple state-versus-market solutions. “A Ladder of Citizen Participation” made participation itself an object of analysis, distinguishing symbolic inclusion from real power. “Making Democracy Work” extended this institutional lineage by linking democratic performance to civic traditions and social capital, helping move comparative politics toward historically rooted, meso-level explanations of governance capacity.

A second lineage concerns how political communities and beliefs are constructed. “Imagined Communities” recast nationalism as a historically produced form of collective imagination rather than a natural political identity. “Framing” connected political communication, sociology, and media studies by clarifying how interpretation structures public meaning. That concern becomes urgent in “Social Media and Fake News in the 2016 Election,” where digital platforms reshape attention, trust, and electoral information. “Planetary boundaries” broadens the field’s horizon again: governance is no longer only about states, citizens, and institutions, but about coordinating human development within Earth-system limits. Together, the cluster connects political science to economics, sociology, environmental science, communication studies, history, and behavioral research, representing a shift from formal authority toward collective action, constructed legitimacy, mediated knowledge, and governance under global constraint.
