# Political science

_10 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

Together, these papers trace political science’s shift from formal institutions and state-centered authority toward the social, ecological, and informational conditions that make collective rule possible. “The Tragedy of the Commons” framed politics as a problem of collective action under scarcity, while “Governing the Commons” overturned simple state-versus-market solutions by showing how communities can build durable institutions for shared resources. “A Ladder of Citizen Participation” made democratic legitimacy depend not only on representation but on the distribution of actual decision power. “Making Democracy Work” extended this institutional turn by linking governmental performance to civic traditions and social capital, while “Imagined Communities” recast nationalism as a historically produced form of collective imagination rather than an ancient or purely ethnic fact.

The cluster’s dominant lineages are therefore participation, collective action, civic culture, nationalism, media framing, and democratic vulnerability. The 1993 paper on framing connected political science to communication, sociology, and psychology by treating public meaning-making as a central mechanism of politics. Later works expand the field’s scale and threat model: “Planetary Boundaries” links governance to Earth-system science, making political order inseparable from ecological limits, while “Social Media and Fake News in the 2016 Election” ties democratic behavior to platform-mediated information environments. Collectively, the papers represent a broadening of political science from government as institutions to politics as coordinated action, shared identity, mediated perception, and survival within social and planetary constraints.
