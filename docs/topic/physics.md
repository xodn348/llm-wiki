# Physics

_92 Fleming-tier papers._

## Synthesis

These Fleming-tier physics papers collectively trace the twentieth-century replacement of classical pictures of matter, radiation, and spacetime with quantum, relativistic, statistical, and computational frameworks. One dominant lineage runs from optical scattering in turbid media through Rutherford’s nuclear atom, Compton scattering, electron diffraction, the positron, many-electron approximations, quantum electrodynamics, density-functional theory, pseudopotentials, and molecular dynamics: matter becomes understood through quantized interactions, field theory, and increasingly computable electronic structure. A second lineage follows nuclei, stars, and gravitation: stellar energy production, nuclear fission, continued gravitational contraction, singularity theorems, cosmic microwave background observations, black-hole entropy, Hawking radiation, and inflation connect microphysics to cosmology and astrophysics.

The major paradigm shifts are not isolated discoveries but changes in explanatory scale. Superconductivity, Josephson tunnelling, Anderson localization, the quantum Hall effect, and low-dimensional magnetism reshaped condensed-matter physics around collective behavior, topology, symmetry, and disorder. Gauge-boson mass, leptons, CP violation, neutrino species, and asymptotic freedom define the modern particle-physics lineage toward the Standard Model. Renormalization group theory, critical phenomena, self-organized criticality, fractals, polymers, turbulence, granular media, and numerical climate circulation show physics exporting methods to complex systems, computation, chemistry, materials science, Earth science, and even biology, as in morphogenesis. The inclusion of *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions* is apt: this cluster is largely a record of physics repeatedly changing its own standards of explanation.
